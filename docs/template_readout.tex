\section{Generator vs GNN}

The generator is a conductance-based network of $N$ neurons. For neuron $i$,
%
\begin{equation}
\tau_i \frac{d\vi}{dt}
   \;=\; -\bigl(\vi - V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i\bigr) \;+\; \msg_i \;+\; I_i ,
\qquad
\msg_i \;=\; \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \Wij \,\act(\vj)\,\bigl(\Eij - \vi\bigr),
\label{eq:gen}
\end{equation}
%
with $\Wij \ge 0$ a synaptic conductance in
units of the leak conductance, and $\Eij$ the reversal potential of the channel
the presynaptic cell opens. The sign of a synapse lives entirely in the driving
force $\Eij - \vi$, never in $\Wij$.

The model is a graph network with the same connectivity:
%
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\vi}{dt} \;=\; f_\theta\!\bigl(\vi, a_i, \widehat{\msg}_i, I_i\bigr),
\qquad
\widehat{\msg}_i \;=\; \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \widehat{W}_{ij}^{\,2}\;
     g_\phi\!\bigl(\vj, a_j, \vi, a_i\bigr),
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
%
where $a_i$ is a learned per-neuron embedding and $\widehat{W}_{ij}$ a learned
per-edge weight.

\paragraph{Why the weight is squared.} It puts the sign where
\eqref{eq:gen} puts it. $\widehat{W}_{ij}^{\,2} \ge 0$ makes the learned weight a
conductance by construction, matching $\Wij \ge 0$, and leaves $g_\phi$ free to
be negative --- which it must be, since the factor it has to reproduce is
$\act(\vj)\,(\Eij - \vi)$ and the driving force is negative on an inhibitory
synapse. The alternative pairing, $\widehat{W}_{ij}\,g_\phi^2$, is the CURRENT
generator's convention, where the true weight is itself signed; used on
conductance data it forbids $g_\phi$ from ever changing sign, so no inhibitory
driving force can be represented and the polarity is pushed into
$\widehat{W}_{ij}$ instead --- against a $\Wij$ that is non-negative by
construction. Exactly one of the two may be squared. Throughout what follows,
$\widehat{W}_{ij}^{\,2}$ is what the per-edge fit of \S\ref{sec:edge} recovers,
because it is what multiplies $g_\phi$ in the message; the raw parameter is its
square root and is never compared with anything.

Only $d\vi/dt$ is supervised. \emph{Nothing in the loss observes
$\widehat{\msg}$}, and that is the whole difficulty: the message is an internal
variable, so any rescaling of it that $f_\theta$ can undo leaves the training
objective exactly unchanged. \S\ref{sec:readout} first measures what the network
computes; \S\ref{sec:gauge} makes that statement precise and undoes it.

\section{The readout, as least squares}
\label{sec:readout}

The question is an empirical one: does the trained network obey the generator's
equation~\eqref{eq:gen} at all, and if so with which constants? The two are answered by the same fit. Impose the
generator's form on what the network computes, ask least squares for the
constants that fit it best, and read the residual: the coefficients are the
recovered parameters, and the $R^2$ is how well the hypothesis holds on that edge
or that neuron. An edge whose message cannot be written as
$\Wij\,\act(\vj)\,(\Eij-\vi)$ reports a low $R^2$ and its constants mean nothing;
one that fits at $0.999$ has a message the generator could have produced, and
then the only question left is whether the constants are the right ones.


\subsection{Per edge: $\Wij$ and $\Eij$}
\label{sec:edge}

Write $u_t = \act(\vj(t))$. The generator's synaptic form, multiplied out, is
%
\begin{equation}
\msg_{ij}(t) \;=\; \Wij\,u_t\,\bigl(\Eij - \vi(t)\bigr) + C_{ij}
  \;=\; \underbrace{(\Wij \Eij)}_{b_1}\,u_t
  \;+\; \underbrace{(-\Wij)}_{b_2}\,\bigl(u_t\,\vi(t)\bigr)
  \;+\; \underbrace{C_{ij}}_{b_3},
\label{eq:edgelin}
\end{equation}
%
linear in $(b_1,b_2,b_3)$. The target is the model's own per-edge message,
$\widehat{\msg}_{ij}(t)$, taken from its forward pass, so the fit asks what
constants the generator would need to produce what the network computes. Stack
the $n$ usable frames $t_1 \dots t_n$ of this edge into a design matrix $A$ and
the model's messages into $y$:
%
\begin{equation}
A \;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
u_{t_1} & u_{t_1}\vi(t_1) & 1\\
u_{t_2} & u_{t_2}\vi(t_2) & 1\\
\vdots  & \vdots          & \vdots\\
u_{t_n} & u_{t_n}\vi(t_n) & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\in \mathbb{R}^{n\times 3},
\qquad
b = \begin{pmatrix} b_1\\ b_2\\ b_3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
y = \begin{pmatrix}
\widehat{\msg}_{ij}(t_1)\\ \vdots\\ \widehat{\msg}_{ij}(t_n)
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:design}
\end{equation}
%
The problem is $\min_b \lVert A b - y\rVert_2^2$, whose normal equations
$A^{\!\top}\!A\,b = A^{\!\top}y$ give
%
\begin{equation}
b \;=\; (A^{\!\top}\! A)^{-1} A^{\!\top} y ,
\qquad
\Wij = -b_2 , \qquad \Eij = -\,b_1/b_2 , \qquad C_{ij} = b_3 .
\label{eq:edgesolve}
\end{equation}
%
$A^{\!\top}\!A$ is $3\times3$ whatever $n$ is, and its six distinct entries are
sums over frames --- $\sum u^2$, $\sum u^2 \vi$, $\sum u$, and so on --- so the
frames stream and nothing of size $n_{\mathrm{edges}} \times n$ is ever held.
All $434{,}112$ edges are then solved together as a batch of $3\times3$ systems,
in seconds.

\paragraph{Which rows are usable.} A row carries information about $\Wij$ and
$\Eij$ only when the presynaptic cell is \emph{on}: below the activity floor
$u_t = 0$, the row is $[0,\,0,\,1]$ and constrains the offset alone. The floor is
not zero but the median of the positive $\act(\vj)$, measured on a small probe, so
that ``on'' means carrying real drive rather than numerical dust. Three unknowns
means eight usable rows are ample, and that threshold, $n_{\min} = 8$, is what the
sampling has to deliver on every edge.

\paragraph{Why a uniform sample will not.} An edge is measurable only on its own
presynaptic cell's active frames, and those cells are not equally busy. A cell
active in $2\%$ of the recording contributes about five rows to a $256$-frame
draw --- below the eight the fit needs --- and such cells are not a fringe: a
quarter of this connectome's edges have one. Drawing four times as many frames
moves that share by about four points, because the extra frames come from the
same distribution, while the sampler materialises an
$n_{\mathrm{edges}} \times n$ array and runs out of memory around $n = 4096$.
Spending more frames uniformly is the one thing that does not work.

\paragraph{Two passes, each aimed at the edges the previous one missed.}

\emph{Pass 1, choosing the frames.} Start from a uniform draw of $n = 256$. Count
how often each presynaptic cell clears the floor in it, and for the cells still
short of eight, add frames drawn from \emph{that cell's own} active frames, up to
a cap of $2048$. The result is a frame set of the same order as the base --- the
busy cells were already covered by it --- that carries the rare cells to the
threshold as well.

\emph{Pass 2, topping up the sums.} The first pass still leaves roughly a quarter
of edges under $n_{\min}$. Rather than re-fit everything, $768$ further frames are
streamed in chunks of $256$ and their contributions added to the ten
normal-equation sums \emph{only where the count is short}. This is possible
because nothing of size $n_{\mathrm{edges}} \times n$ survives the accumulation:
$A^{\!\top}\!A$ and $A^{\!\top}y$ are sums over frames, so a second sample simply
adds to them. Edges already well determined are untouched, so their constants
cannot move.

Together the two passes fit $99.6\%$ of the $434{,}112$ edges. Both are reported
rather than assumed --- \texttt{tmpl\_pct\_unfitted\_first\_pass} and
\texttt{tmpl\_pct\_rescued\_second\_pass} say how much of that came from each ---
because the edges the second pass admits are by construction the MARGINAL ones,
and buying coverage with them is not free: they arrive with the fewest rows and
the weakest drive, which is exactly the population $\Eij$ is least identifiable
on (\S\ref{sec:Ecorr}).

\paragraph{Why $\Eij$ is the fragile one.} $\Wij$ is a coefficient; $\Eij$ is a
ratio of two, so its relative error is
$(\sigma_E/E)^2 \approx (\sigma_{b_1}/b_1)^2 + (\sigma_{b_2}/b_2)^2$ and diverges
as $b_2 \to 0$. And $b_2$ is small not when the synapse is weak but when the
postsynaptic voltage barely moves: if $\vi(t) \approx c$ over the usable frames
then $u_t \vi(t) \approx c\,u_t$, the two columns are nearly the same vector,
and only the combination $b_1 + c\,b_2$ is determined. The guard is a $t$-test
on that coefficient, from the same normal equations:
%
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{se}(b_2) = \sqrt{\hat\sigma^2 \bigl[(A^{\!\top}\!A)^{-1}\bigr]_{22}},
\qquad
t = \frac{|b_2|}{\mathrm{se}(b_2)},
\qquad
\Eij \;\text{kept only where } t \ge 3 .
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
%
Everything below it is recorded as unidentified rather than dropped silently. On
the conductance run used throughout: $432{,}517$ of the $434{,}112$ edges are
fitted ($99.6\%$), and of those, $405{,}503$ clear the gate and report a reversal
($93.4\%$ of all edges).

\subsection{Per neuron: the update fit, and the gauge it leaves free}
\label{sec:update}
\label{sec:gauge}

$f_\theta$ is an MLP: there is no $\tau$ in it to read off, no resting potential,
no message gain. What it is, on this data, is close to affine in its inputs --- so
fit it. Per neuron, taking $f(I)$ linear, regress the model's own $d\vi/dt$ on its
own inputs:
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\frac{d\vi}{dt}} \;=\;
   \alpha_0 \;+\; \alpha_1\,\vi \;+\; \alpha_2\,\widehat{\msg}_i
   \;+\; \alpha_3\,I_i ,
\label{eq:updatesolve}
\end{equation}
%
again $b = (A^{\!\top}\!A)^{-1}A^{\!\top}y$, now $4\times4$ per neuron and
accumulated over frames so nothing of size $N \times n_{\mathrm{frames}}$ is ever
held. Greek, so the coefficients cannot be confused with the per-neuron embedding
$a_i$ of \eqref{eq:model}. The fit's $R^2$ is reported: it is how far the model's
update is from affine in the message, and a low value invalidates all four
coefficients together.

\paragraph{What the generator's coefficients would be.} Divide \eqref{eq:gen} by
$\tau_i$ and it is already of exactly this form,
%
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\vi}{dt} \;=\; \frac{V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i}{\tau_i}
   \;-\; \frac{1}{\tau_i}\,\vi
   \;+\; \frac{1}{\tau_i}\,\msg_i
   \;+\; \frac{1}{\tau_i}\,I_i ,
\label{eq:genexpanded}
\end{equation}
%
so a network obeying it exactly would return
%
\begin{equation}
\alpha_0 = \frac{V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i}{\tau_i}, \qquad
\alpha_1 = -\frac{1}{\tau_i}, \qquad
\alpha_2 = +\frac{1}{\tau_i}, \qquad
\alpha_3 = \frac{1}{\tau_i} .
\label{eq:gencoeffs}
\end{equation}
%
\eqref{eq:gencoeffs} holds in \emph{one} gauge, and \eqref{eq:gaugetransform}
below is what breaks it: that transformation moves $\alpha_0$ and $\alpha_2$ ---
precisely the two coefficients \eqref{eq:gencoeffs} pins --- while leaving
$d\vi/dt$ untouched, so a network obeying the generator exactly returns
\eqref{eq:gencoeffs} only if it happens to sit at $k_i = 1$, $\beta_i = 0$.
What survives is $\alpha_1 = -1/\tau_i$, which \eqref{eq:gaugetransform} does not
touch: the time constant is the one thing readable off the fit with no gauge
argument at all.

\paragraph{The redundancy.} Rescale and shift a neuron's message, and absorb it
in the two coefficients that multiply and offset it:
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\msg}_i \;\longmapsto\; \frac{1}{c}\,\widehat{\msg}_i + d,
\qquad
\alpha_2 \;\longmapsto\; c\,\alpha_2,
\qquad
\alpha_0 \;\longmapsto\; \alpha_0 - c\,\alpha_2\,d ,
\label{eq:gaugetransform}
\end{equation}
%
leaving $d\vi/dt$, hence the loss, unchanged for any $c \ne 0$ and any $d$:
training cannot prefer one member of this two-parameter family over another.

Which member the trained model ended up as is then a question about \emph{this}
model, and the readout's first job is to answer it. Suppose the model's message
is the true one seen through such a transformation:
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\msg}_i \;=\; \frac{1}{k_i}\,\msg_i \;+\; \beta_i .
\label{eq:ansatz}
\end{equation}
%
That is \eqref{eq:gaugetransform} at $c = k_i$, $d = \beta_i$: different letters
because $(c, d)$ are free, while $(k_i, \beta_i)$ are the particular pair
relating this one model to the truth, per neuron, and unknown until measured.
It is a supposition and it can be false --- a message off the family is one whose
per-edge form is not the generator's at all, which is \S\ref{sec:Ecorr} --- so it
is tested by the per-edge $R^2$ rather than assumed. Either \emph{fix} the gauge
during training, where \texttt{coeff\_f\_theta\_msg\_gain} drives $k_i \to 1$ and
\texttt{coeff\_g\_phi\_silent} drives $\beta_i \to 0$, or \emph{undo} it
afterwards by measuring $(k_i, \beta_i)$. This document does the second.

Substituting \eqref{eq:ansatz} into the fit \eqref{eq:updatesolve} puts the
model's update in terms of the \emph{true} message,
%
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\vi}{dt} \;=\;
   \bigl(\alpha_0 + \alpha_2 \beta_i\bigr)
   \;+\; \alpha_1\,\vi
   \;+\; \frac{\alpha_2}{k_i}\,\msg_i
   \;+\; \alpha_3\,I_i ,
\label{eq:updateexpanded}
\end{equation}
%
which is the generator's own form \eqref{eq:genexpanded}. Both hold on every
trajectory the network produces, so their coefficients must agree one by one, and
matching them against \eqref{eq:gencoeffs} gives three identities:
%
\begin{align}
\text{leak:}    && \alpha_1 &= -\frac{1}{\tau_i}
   &&\Longrightarrow&& \tau_i = -1/\alpha_1 ,
   \label{eq:tauid}\\[2pt]
\text{message:} && \frac{\alpha_2}{k_i} &= \frac{1}{\tau_i}
   &&\Longrightarrow&& k_i = \tau_i\,\alpha_2 = -\alpha_2/\alpha_1 ,
   \label{eq:kid}\\[2pt]
\text{constant:}&& \alpha_0 + \alpha_2 \beta_i &= \frac{V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i}{\tau_i}
   &&\Longrightarrow&& V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i
        = \tau_i\bigl(\alpha_0 + \alpha_2 \beta_i\bigr) .
   \label{eq:vrestid}
\end{align}
%
\eqref{eq:kid} is worth reading twice: $k_i = 1$ exactly when
$\alpha_2 = -\alpha_1$, which is the generator's constraint from
\eqref{eq:gencoeffs}. The gauge is fixed precisely when the model's two middle
coefficients are equal and opposite --- which is what
\texttt{coeff\_f\_theta\_msg\_gain} charges during training, and it needs no
$\tau$ to do it.
%

\paragraph{Applying them.} The two fits return numbers about the model: per edge
$(b_1,b_2,b_3)$ and from them $\Wij^{\mathrm{fit}}$, $\Eij$, $C_{ij}$; per neuron
$(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)$. The identities
\eqref{eq:tauid}--\eqref{eq:vrestid} are what turn those into the generator's
parameters, and they are used in this order:
%
\begin{align}
\text{\eqref{eq:tauid}} && \tau_i &= -1/\alpha_1
  && \text{the time constant, directly},\\[2pt]
\text{\eqref{eq:kid}}   && k_i &= -\alpha_2/\alpha_1
  && \text{the message scale, which $\Wij$ is in units of},\\[2pt]
\text{\eqref{eq:vrestid}} && V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i
   &= \tau_i\bigl(\alpha_0 + \alpha_2 \beta_i\bigr)
  && \text{the resting level, offset included}.
\end{align}
%
So $\tau$ comes straight out of the update fit, $\Wij$ needs the scale from
\eqref{eq:kid} applied to the edge fit, and $V^{\mathrm{rest}}$ needs the offset
that \eqref{eq:vrestid} says the update hid in it --- the two corrections below.
$\Eij$ needs neither, for the reason given in \S\ref{sec:Ecorr}; what it needs
instead is a caveat, because the one error it is exposed to is the one no
correction can remove.

\paragraph{$\Wij$: divide by the scale.} By \eqref{eq:kid} the message is in
units of $1/k_i$, and the fitted $b_2$ inherits that, so the weight in the
generator's units is
%
\begin{equation}
\Wij^{\mathrm{corrected}} \;=\; k_i \; \Wij^{\mathrm{fit}}
  \;=\; -\frac{\alpha_2}{\alpha_1} \; \bigl(-b_2\bigr),
\label{eq:wcorr}
\end{equation}
%
both coefficients being the postsynaptic neuron's own, from
\eqref{eq:updatesolve}. Without this the same
weights score $R^2 = -2.14$ where the corrected ones score $0.995$ --- the
uncorrected number is kept as \texttt{Wij\_R2\_uncorrected} so the difference is
visible rather than assumed.

\paragraph{$V^{\mathrm{rest}}$: add the offset back.} By \eqref{eq:vrestid} the
level the update fit reads is short by exactly the neuron's own incoming message
offset:
%
\begin{equation}
V^{\mathrm{rest},\,\mathrm{corrected}}_i
  \;=\; \tau_i\Bigl[\,\underbrace{\alpha_0}_{\text{regression \eqref{eq:updatesolve}}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\alpha_2 \textstyle\sum_{j} C_{ij}}
        _{C_{ij} = b_3 \text{ of \eqref{eq:edgesolve}}}\Bigr] ,
\label{eq:vcorr}
\end{equation}
%

\subsection{$\Eij$: why there is no correction, and what replaces one}
\label{sec:Ecorr}

\paragraph{The scale $k_i$ divides out. The substitution, step by step.} Start
from the ansatz \eqref{eq:ansatz}, which is a statement about neuron $i$'s
\emph{total} incoming message:
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\msg}_i \;=\; \frac{1}{k_i}\,\msg_i \;+\; \beta_i
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\msg_i = \sum_j \msg_{ij}, \quad \widehat{\msg}_i = \sum_j \widehat{\msg}_{ij} .
\tag{\ref{eq:ansatz}}
\end{equation}
%
\emph{Step 1 --- take it down to one edge.} The fit of \S\ref{sec:edge} works on
a single synapse, so the ansatz has to be read per edge. Assume the model applies
the same scale $k_i$ to every edge into $i$ and splits the offset among them,
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\msg}_{ij}(t) \;=\; \frac{1}{k_i}\,\msg_{ij}(t) \;+\; C_{ij},
\qquad \sum_j C_{ij} = \beta_i .
\label{eq:edgeansatz}
\end{equation}
%
$k_i$ is a property of the receiving neuron, so carrying one scale across its
edges is forced; splitting $\beta_i$ into per-edge constants is not, and that
assumption is exactly what the next paragraph removes.

\emph{Step 2 --- put the generator's synapse in.} The true per-edge message is
$\msg_{ij}(t) = \Wij\,u_t\,(\Eij - \vi(t))$ with $u_t = \act(\vj(t))$, so
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\msg}_{ij}(t)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{k_i}\,\Wij\,u_t\,\bigl(\Eij - \vi(t)\bigr) \;+\; C_{ij}.
\end{equation}
%
\emph{Step 3 --- multiply out the bracket.} Distributing $u_t$ over
$\Eij - \vi(t)$ separates the two things that multiply it:
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\msg}_{ij}(t)
  \;=\; \frac{\Wij \Eij}{k_i}\;u_t
  \;-\; \frac{\Wij}{k_i}\;\bigl(u_t\,\vi(t)\bigr)
  \;+\; C_{ij}.
\label{eq:Eexpand}
\end{equation}
%
\emph{Step 4 --- read off the coefficients.} The fit \eqref{eq:edgelin} regresses
$\widehat{\msg}_{ij}$ on the three columns $u_t$, $u_t \vi(t)$ and $1$. Equation
\eqref{eq:Eexpand} is already in that form, so its three coefficients \emph{are}
$b_1, b_2, b_3$:
%
\begin{equation}
b_1 = \frac{\Wij \Eij}{k_i}, \qquad
b_2 = -\frac{\Wij}{k_i}, \qquad
b_3 = C_{ij}.
\label{eq:Ecoeffs}
\end{equation}
%
\emph{Step 5 --- divide.} Both $b_1$ and $b_2$ carry the same factor $1/k_i$,
and \eqref{eq:edgesolve} takes their ratio:
%
\begin{equation}
-\frac{b_1}{b_2}
  \;=\; -\,\frac{\Wij \Eij / k_i}{-\Wij / k_i}
  \;=\; \frac{\Wij \Eij}{\Wij}\cdot\frac{k_i}{k_i}
  \;=\; \Eij \ \ \text{exactly, for any } k_i .
\end{equation}
%
$\Wij$ is a single coefficient and keeps the $1/k_i$, which is why
\eqref{eq:wcorr} multiplies it back; $\Eij$ is a \emph{ratio} of two coefficients
that carry the same factor, so it divides out. The offset cannot reach $\Eij$
either --- $C_{ij}$ is $b_3$, and $b_3$ appears in neither $b_1$ nor $b_2$. That
is the precise content of ``$\Eij$ needs neither correction''.

\paragraph{But $\Eij$ is exposed to a contamination $\Wij$ is not.} Step 1 above
is where the whole result rests, and it is an assumption. The ansatz
\eqref{eq:ansatz} is a statement about neuron $i$'s \emph{total} message; writing
it per edge as \eqref{eq:edgeansatz} adds the claim that whatever the model does
beyond rescaling is a per-edge \emph{constant}. Nothing in the training
objective requires that. Relax it in the one direction that matters --- let each
edge also carry a component proportional to the drive, with no driving force in
it:
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\msg}_{ij}(t) \;=\; \frac{1}{k_i}\,\Wij\,u_t\,\bigl(\Eij - \vi(t)\bigr)
   \;+\; C_{ij} \;+\; D_{ij}\,u_t .
\label{eq:contam}
\end{equation}
%
$D_{ij} u_t$ is exactly the \emph{current} family's form, $W \act(\vj)$, and it
is collinear with the $\Wij\Eij$ term: both live in the first column of $A$. The
fit therefore returns
%
\begin{equation}
b_1 = \frac{\Wij\Eij}{k_i} + D_{ij}, \qquad b_2 = -\frac{\Wij}{k_i},
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\Wij^{\mathrm{corrected}} = \Wij \ \ \text{(untouched)}, \qquad
\Eij^{\mathrm{fit}} = \Eij + \frac{k_i D_{ij}}{\Wij}.
\label{eq:Ebias}
\end{equation}
%
So a current-form component leaves the conductance \emph{exactly} right and
biases the reversal, by an amount that grows as $\Wij$ shrinks. This is not a
gauge and there is no correction for it: $b_1$ is one column's coefficient and
cannot be split into $\Wij\Eij$ and $k_i D_{ij}$ from a single edge's message.
The two are unidentifiable together, and any procedure claiming to separate them
is using an assumption from outside the data.

\paragraph{What is reported instead.} Two numbers, which separate the two ways
$\Eij$ can be wrong. The \emph{variance} failure --- $b_2$ small because $\vi$
barely moved, so its reciprocal explodes --- is handled by the $t$-gate
\eqref{eq:gate} and quantified by the delta-method standard error of the ratio,
propagated through both coefficients off the same $(A^{\!\top}\!A)^{-1}$:
%
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{var}(\Eij) \;=\; \frac{\hat\sigma^2}{b_2^{\,2}}
  \Bigl[ V_{11} + \Bigl(\frac{b_1}{b_2}\Bigr)^{\!2} V_{22}
         - 2\,\frac{b_1}{b_2}\,V_{12} \Bigr],
\qquad V = (A^{\!\top}\!A)^{-1} .
\label{eq:seE}
\end{equation}
%
The \emph{bias} failure of \eqref{eq:Ebias} is invisible to that: it shifts the
estimate without widening it. It shows up only as an observed error far larger
than \eqref{eq:seE} predicts, which is what the ratio
$\mathrm{median}|\Eij^{\mathrm{fit}} - \Eij| \big/ \mathrm{median}\,
\mathrm{se}(\Eij)$ measures. Near $1$, the reversal is recovered as well as the
data allows and more frames would help. Far above $1$, the message carries a
term the conductance form has no column for, and more frames will not move it.
Both are reported per run as \texttt{Eij\_se\_median} and
\texttt{Eij\_err\_over\_se}, beside the two-form comparison
(\texttt{current\_form\_r2\_median} against
\texttt{conductance\_form\_r2\_median}) that says how much the driving-force
column was worth in the first place.

\paragraph{A consequence worth stating plainly.} $\Wij$ and $\Eij$ fail
independently and for opposite reasons. $\Wij$ is immune to the contamination
\eqref{eq:contam} but carries the gauge parameter $k_i$, so it is wrong by one
global factor whenever the message scale is unpinned. $\Eij$ is immune to $k_i$
but not to the contamination. A run reporting a good $\Eij$ and a $\Wij$ off by a
uniform factor has an unfixed-gauge problem; one reporting a good $\Wij$ and a
scattered
$\Eij$ has a message whose form is not purely conductance. The two diagnoses are
not interchangeable, and neither is read off the other's number.

\paragraph{The $V^{\mathrm{rest}}$ identity closes.} Equation~\eqref{eq:vrestid}
predicts $V^{\mathrm{rest}} = \tau\alpha_0 + \tau\alpha_2\beta$, and the second
term is the fitted offset of panel (c). Substituting: $-0.083 + 2.334 = +2.251$
against the generator's $+2.910$ --- $77\%$ of the resting potential recovered,
of which essentially all comes from the message's offset and almost none from
$\tau\alpha_0 = -0.083$. Reading $\tau\alpha_0$ alone as $V^{\mathrm{rest}}$,
which is what dropping the $\alpha_2\beta$ term amounts to, would be wrong by
$3$\,V.

\paragraph{The per-edge offsets sum to the per-neuron one.} Panel (c) reports
$+2.334$ measured on the total message against $+1.510$ from summing the eight
fitted $C_{ij}$; the gap is the three synapses the model pruned, which have no
fit to contribute one. The two stages agree on a quantity neither of them
imposes.
