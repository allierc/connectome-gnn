\title{Reading a connectome out of a graph network:\\
       the template readout and the gauge it has to undo}
\author{}
\date{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A graph network trained to predict neural dynamics can reproduce every trajectory
while getting every synapse wrong, because only the sum of a neuron's own
dynamics and its incoming message is observed. This note writes down the
transformation that freedom permits --- it is affine, not a pure scale --- and
gives a closed-form readout that inverts it: fit the generator's own synaptic
form to the model's per-edge message, then carry the result into the generator's
units with a factor measured from the model's update. Every step is a least
squares with an explicit solution, so the whole $434{,}112$-edge connectome is
read in seconds. On a conductance network trained at process noise $\sigma=0.05$
the readout recovers the median synaptic conductance to $7.3\%$ and raises
cell-type clustering accuracy from $0.490$ to $0.583$; it also shows, and
quantifies, the two failures that remain.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is observed, and what is free}

The generator is a conductance-based network of $N$ neurons. For neuron $i$,
%
\begin{equation}
\tau_i \frac{d\vi}{dt}
   \;=\; -\bigl(\vi - V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i\bigr) \;+\; \msg_i \;+\; I_i ,
\qquad
\msg_i \;=\; \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \Wij \,\act(\vj)\,\bigl(\Eij - \vi\bigr),
\label{eq:gen}
\end{equation}
%
with $\act = \mathrm{relu}$ on this data, $\Wij \ge 0$ a synaptic conductance in
units of the leak conductance, and $\Eij$ the reversal potential of the channel
the presynaptic cell opens. The sign of a synapse lives entirely in the driving
force $\Eij - \vi$, never in $\Wij$.

The model is a graph network with the same connectivity:
%
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\vi}{dt} \;=\; f_\theta\!\bigl(\vi, a_i, \widehat{\msg}_i, I_i\bigr),
\qquad
\widehat{\msg}_i \;=\; \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \widehat{W}_{ij}\;
     g_\phi\!\bigl(\vj, a_j, \vi, a_i\bigr),
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
%
where $a_i$ is a learned per-neuron embedding and $\widehat{W}_{ij}$ a learned
per-edge weight. Only $d\vi/dt$ is supervised. \emph{Nothing in the loss observes
$\widehat{\msg}$}, and that is the whole difficulty: the message is an internal
variable, and any transformation of it that $f_\theta$ can undo leaves the
training objective exactly unchanged.

\subsection{The gauge is affine}

Suppose the model's message is related to the truth by
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\msg}_i \;=\; \frac{1}{k_i}\,\msg_i \;+\; \beta_i .
\label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
%
Write the model's update in the same form as the generator's, which is what
the update fit of \S\ref{sec:update} measures:
%
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\vi}{dt} \;=\; T_i\Bigl[\bigl(V_i - \vi\bigr) + G_i \widehat{\msg}_i
                              + f(I_i)\Bigr].
\label{eq:updatetmpl}
\end{equation}
%
Substituting \eqref{eq:gauge} into \eqref{eq:updatetmpl} and matching
coefficients against \eqref{eq:gen} term by term gives three identities:
%
\begin{align}
\text{message:} && T_i G_i \frac{1}{k_i} &= \frac{1}{\tau_i}
   &&\Longrightarrow&& k_i = \tau_i\, T_i\, G_i ,
   \label{eq:kid}\\[2pt]
\text{leak:}    && T_i &= \frac{1}{\tau_i}
   &&\Longrightarrow&& \tau_i = 1/T_i ,
   \label{eq:tauid}\\[2pt]
\text{constant:}&& T_i V_i + T_i G_i \beta_i &= \frac{V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i}{\tau_i}
   &&\Longrightarrow&& V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i = \tau_i T_i V_i + k_i \beta_i .
   \label{eq:vrestid}
\end{align}
%
Three consequences, and they are the reason this note exists.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The scale $k_i$ is unobservable.} Equation~\eqref{eq:kid} is
satisfied for \emph{any} $k_i$, by pairing it with $G_i = k_i/(\tau_i T_i)$. A
model whose messages are $25\times$ too large and whose $f_\theta$ divides by
$25$ predicts precisely the same dynamics as the truth.
\item \textbf{The offset $\beta_i$ is unobservable too, and it is not harmless:}
by \eqref{eq:vrestid} it is absorbed into the resting potential. A constant in
the message and a shift of $V^{\mathrm{rest}}$ are the same function.
\item \textbf{The reversal $\Eij$ is gauge-invariant.} It is the voltage at which
a synapse's message vanishes --- a root --- and a root does not move when the
function around it is rescaled or, per edge, shifted by a constant that the fit
accounts for separately. This is why $\Eij$ can be recovered absolutely while
$\Wij$ cannot be, without further work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The readout}

The readout inverts \eqref{eq:gauge} in three stages: fit the generator's
synaptic form to each edge (\S\ref{sec:edge}), fit the generator's update form
to each neuron (\S\ref{sec:update}), and combine them (\S\ref{sec:gauge}).

\subsection{Stage 1: the per-edge template}
\label{sec:edge}

For every edge $(i,j)$ and every sampled frame $t$ we have the model's own
per-edge message, which is \eqref{eq:model} before the sum,
%
\begin{equation}
m_{ij}(t) \;=\; \widehat{W}_{ij}\; g_\phi\bigl(\vj(t), a_j, \vi(t), a_i\bigr).
\end{equation}
%
We ask what conductance, reversal and offset best explain it inside the
generator's own form. Writing $u_{ij}(t) = \act\bigl(\vj(t)\bigr)$ --- the
generator's activation, \emph{given}, not fitted ---
%
\begin{equation}
m_{ij} \;\approx\; \Wij\, u_{ij}\,\bigl(\Eij - \vi\bigr) \;+\; C_{ij}
     \;=\; \underbrace{\Wij \Eij}_{b_1}\, u_{ij}
       \;+\; \underbrace{(-\Wij)}_{b_2}\, u_{ij}\vi
       \;+\; \underbrace{C_{ij}}_{b_3}\cdot 1 .
\label{eq:edgefit}
\end{equation}
%
With $\act$ given, \eqref{eq:edgefit} is \emph{linear in its three unknowns}:
a three-column least squares with a closed-form solution, one per edge, solved
for all edges at once from stacked normal equations. This is the step that would
otherwise be a symbolic regression --- searching for $\act$ as well costs about
$300$\,s per \emph{neuron}, against seconds for the entire connectome here.

Three details matter.

\paragraph{The offset $C_{ij}$ is a measurement, not a nuisance.} The generator's
per-edge message is identically zero whenever the presynaptic cell is silent, for
every edge and whatever the postsynaptic state; $g_\phi$ is under no such
obligation. $C_{ij}$ is exactly what it emits there, and by \eqref{eq:vrestid}
the incoming sum $\sum_j C_{ij}$ is what neuron $i$'s resting potential absorbs.
Fitting it also unbiases the other two coefficients: without a constant in the
model, a message sitting on a pedestal is fitted by tilting the driving force
instead, which moves the reversal.

\paragraph{Frames with no drive carry no information.} Only frames with
$u_{ij}$ above the median of the positive activations enter the fit; below that
the driving force is multiplied by almost nothing.

\paragraph{The reversal is reported only where it is identified.} Since
$\Eij = b_1 / (-b_2)$ divides by the second coefficient, an edge whose
postsynaptic voltage barely moved returns the reciprocal of a small noisy
number. The edge reports a reversal only when
%
\begin{equation}
\frac{|b_2|}{\mathrm{se}(b_2)} \;\ge\; t^\ast, \qquad
\mathrm{se}(b_2)^2 = \frac{\mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{res}}}{n-3}\,
   \bigl[(X^\top X)^{-1}\bigr]_{22},
\end{equation}
%
with $t^\ast = 3$. Before this gate, a handful of such edges drove the reversal
RMSE to $571$\,V.

\paragraph{The conductance comes from the better-determined coefficient.} The
generator gives every edge onto neuron $i$ one of exactly two reversals --- one
for the excitatory senders, one for the inhibitory --- so the surviving per-edge
estimates of each sign are repeated measurements of one number and their median
$\bar{E}_i^{\pm}$ is the estimator that says so. Then
%
\begin{equation}
\widehat{W}^{\,\mathrm{fit}}_{ij} \;=\; \frac{b_1}{\bar{E}_i^{\pm}}
\qquad\text{rather than}\qquad
\widehat{W}^{\,\mathrm{fit}}_{ij} = -b_2 ,
\end{equation}
%
because $b_1$ multiplies the drive itself while $b_2$ multiplies
$u_{ij}\vi$ and is only as good as the postsynaptic voltage's excursion.

\subsection{Stage 2: the per-neuron update}
\label{sec:update}

Equation~\eqref{eq:updatetmpl} is likewise linear once $f(I)$ is taken linear:
%
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\vi}{dt} \;\approx\; a_0 + a_1 \vi + a_2 \widehat{\msg}_i + a_3 I_i ,
\qquad
T_i = -a_1, \quad V_i = \frac{a_0}{T_i}, \quad G_i = \frac{a_2}{T_i},
\label{eq:updatefit}
\end{equation}
%
a four-column least squares per neuron, accumulated over frames as
$(N,4,4)$ normal equations so that nothing of size $(N, n_{\mathrm{frames}})$ is
ever held. The quality of this linearisation is reported: on the example run the
median per-neuron $R^2$ of \eqref{eq:updatefit} is $0.9994$, and the fitted
$T_iG_i$ agrees with an autograd evaluation of $\partial f_\theta/\partial \msg$
to a median ratio of $0.9994$ --- the two coincide exactly when the update is
affine in the message, so their ratio is a check that it is.

One confound must be named: a constant inside $f(I)$ reaches $d\vi/dt$ as a
constant times $T_i$, exactly as $V_i$ does, so on neurons that receive a
stimulus the two cannot be separated. On this data that is the photoreceptors
only.

\subsection{Stage 3: the gauge}
\label{sec:gauge}

From \eqref{eq:kid}, each neuron's conductances are carried into the generator's
units by
%
\begin{equation}
k_i \;=\; \tau_i\, T_i\, G_i ,
\qquad
\Wij^{\,\mathrm{learned}} \;=\; k_i \; \widehat{W}^{\,\mathrm{fit}}_{ij},
\qquad
\Eij^{\,\mathrm{learned}} \;=\; \Eij^{\,\mathrm{fit}} .
\label{eq:apply}
\end{equation}
%
Which $\tau_i$ closes this conversion is a genuine methodological choice and the
note takes a position on it.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Blind (default).} Use the model's own $\tau_i = 1/T_i$ from
\eqref{eq:tauid}, whereupon $k_i = G_i$ exactly. Nothing from the generator
enters, which is required of a recovery metric: $\tau$ is one of the quantities
being recovered and cannot appear in its own readout.
\item \textbf{Diagnostic.} Use the generator's $\tau_i$. This is \emph{not} a
recovery number --- it uses the answer --- but it separates the template's own
error from $\tau$'s, because $W^{\mathrm{true}}$ is written in units where the
message enters $d\vi/dt$ as $\msg/\tau^{\mathrm{true}}$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
The difference between them is large and is itself a result: see
\S\ref{sec:population}.

\section{A worked example}

Figure~\ref{fig:panels} shows the readout applied to neuron $2895$ of a
conductance network trained at process noise $\sigma=0.05$ on the flyvis
connectome ($N = 13{,}741$ neurons, $434{,}112$ edges).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{neuron2895_panels.png}
\caption{One neuron, term by term. \textbf{(a)}~free-running rollout, generator
in green and model in black, $r = 0.998$. \textbf{(b)}~$d v/dt$ at the true
voltages, $R^2 = 0.997$. \textbf{(c)}~the total incoming message: the learned one
after the affine correction of \eqref{eq:gauge}, against the generator's. The
heading reports the fitted scale $0.0844$ against $T G \tau = 0.0923$ from panel
(e) --- two independent routes to the same number, agreeing to $9\%$ --- and the
offset $b = -2.334$\,V. \textbf{(d)}~each of the $11$ synapses separately, mean
removed, on one shared voltage scale so that a synapse the model dropped looks
flat rather than being magnified to full height. \textbf{(e)}~the update fit of
\eqref{eq:updatefit}: $T = 14.79$ against the generator's $1/\tau = 11.11$,
$G = 0.0693$ against a target of $1$. \textbf{(f)}~per synapse, the generator's
$(W, E, \text{offset}=0)$ against the template fit of \eqref{eq:edgefit}, and a
free symbolic search for comparison.}
\label{fig:panels}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The $V^{\mathrm{rest}}$ identity closes.} Equation~\eqref{eq:vrestid}
predicts $V^{\mathrm{rest}} = \tau T V - b$ where $b = -k\beta$ is the fitted
offset of panel (c). Substituting: $-0.083 + 2.334 = +2.251$ against the
generator's $+2.910$ --- $77\%$ of the resting potential recovered, of which
essentially all comes from the message's offset and almost none from the
update's own level $V = -0.21$. Reading $V$ alone as $V^{\mathrm{rest}}$, as a
naive fit of \eqref{eq:updatetmpl} invites, would be wrong by $3$\,V.

\paragraph{The per-edge offsets sum to the per-neuron one.} Panel (c) reports
$+2.334$ measured on the total message against $+1.510$ from summing the eight
fitted $C_{ij}$; the gap is the three synapses the model pruned, which have no
fit to contribute one. The two stages agree on a quantity neither of them
imposes.

Three readings of Table~\ref{tab:pop} deserve emphasis.

\paragraph{The form is identified; the constant inside it is not.} The median
per-edge $R^2$ of the conductance template is $0.9997$: the model's message
\emph{is} of the form $W\act(\vj)(E-\vi) + C$ to four decimal places. The
reversal implied by that form nonetheless has an RMSE of $7.76$\,V across the
$66$ distinct values the generator uses. Reporting the fit quality as if it were
recovery would be reporting the extraction's goodness of fit; both numbers
belong in any claim about reversal potentials.

\paragraph{What separates blind from diagnostic is $\tau$, and only $\tau$.}
The two columns differ by nothing but which time constant closes
\eqref{eq:apply}. Since $\tau$ is recovered at $R^2 = 0.925$, one might expect
the effect to be small; it is not, because $k_i$ multiplies an entire row of the
connectome, so each neuron's $\tau$ error scales all of its synapses coherently.
The median $\tau$ is $37\%$ too large, and the $W$ statistics inherit exactly
that. \emph{This makes a prediction that can falsify the whole framing}: a
regulariser that pins $G_i \to 1$ forces $k_i = \tau_i T_i G_i \to 1$ and removes
$\tau$ from the conductance readout altogether, so the two columns should
converge. If they do not, the gauge is not the whole story.

\paragraph{The population number is dominated by synapses that do not exist.}
Figure~\ref{fig:scatter} shows why the per-neuron picture
(median learned/true $1.39$, interquartile range $1.22$) and the population $R^2$
($0.273$) tell different stories. Half the fitted edges have a true
conductance below $10^{-8}$ (the first five deciles run from $2\times10^{-15}$ to
$8.5\times10^{-9}$) --- the generator has no synapse
there --- and the model gives them conductances up to $10^{-2}$. They dominate
the sum of squares while carrying no biology. A second, smaller population is
whole postsynaptic neurons whose rows collapsed, visible as vertical streaks at
ratios of $10^{-6}$ and below.

\section{Is the method sound?}

\paragraph{What it assumes.} (i)~That $\act$ is known. It is given from the
generator, so on real data this becomes an assumption about the synaptic
nonlinearity and should be stated as one; the free symbolic search in panel (f)
of Figure~\ref{fig:panels} exists to check it, and a separate PySR template
search --- which fits $\act$ rather than assuming it --- is run on a handful of
neurons for exactly that purpose. (ii)~That $f_\theta$ is approximately affine in
the message over the observed range; measured, not assumed
($R^2 = 0.9994$, ratio to autograd $0.9994$). (iii)~That the generator gives each
neuron two reversals, used only by the pooling step, which can be disabled.

\paragraph{What it does not do.} It does not make an unidentifiable quantity
identifiable. The offset $\beta_i$ is measured here against the generator's
message, which is an \emph{account} of the error, not a blind recovery; only a
prior that pins the message's level --- a penalty on $g_\phi$ at silent
presynaptic input --- makes it identifiable during training. The same holds for
the scale: the readout undoes $k_i$ after the fact, and a penalty tying
$\partial f_\theta/\partial \msg$ to $-\partial f_\theta/\partial v$ is what
would remove it during training.

\paragraph{What would falsify it.} The convergence prediction of
\S\ref{sec:population}; the agreement of $\sum_j C_{ij}$ with the independently
fitted total offset, which currently holds to the pruned synapses; and the
agreement of the fitted $T_iG_i$ with autograd, which would break first if the
update stopped being affine in the message.

\section{Implementation}

The readout is \texttt{metrics.extract\_template\_params}, scored through the
same \texttt{score\_recovery} as every other estimator so that the two readouts
of a run differ only in \texttt{Wij\_estimator} and can be compared line by line.
Per-edge fits are batched \texttt{numpy} solves of stacked $3\times3$ normal
equations; the per-neuron update is a batched $4\times4$ solve accumulated over
frames. Cost on the example run: all $434{,}112$ edges and $13{,}741$ neurons in
under a minute on one L4, against the $\sim 300$\,s per neuron a symbolic search
over $\act$ would take. The figures in this note are generated by
\texttt{GNN\_Main.py -o plot} and \texttt{tools/wij\_scatter.py}.
