\title{\textbf{Zebrafish anterior hindbrain / dIPN: integrating heading
direction, forward velocity, and 2-D position}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
How much computation can a circuit with a measured connectome support?
We test this on a vertebrate
self-motion integrator at single-neuron resolution --- the GABAergic
\rpi{} population of the larval-zebrafish anterior hindbrain and the
afferent populations the \emph{fish2} EM inventory identifies as
carrying its angular and translational drives. From the
\emph{neuprint-fish2} reconstruction we build sign-locked RNN and
message-passing GNN models whose recurrent connectivity is fixed to the
connectome under an all-inhibitory Dale convention, and train the
\emph{same} wiring on a family of progressively harder integration
tasks --- heading, heading $+$ forward distance, 2-D path integration,
and a proprioceptive sensory-vs-efference gain mismatch --- to ask what
the wiring \emph{can} compute and how much the data actually pin down.
Optimising the input--output map alone recovers a sharp heading code on
every task, but the implementation is degenerate: the preferred-heading
map is not anatomically organised, many connectome-consistent circuits
integrate equally well, and the recurrent pool mixes the angular and
translational codes rather than segregating them by cell type. The
task-only model's population activity is also far from the measured
ZAPBench calcium; adding a calcium-observation loss that matches
predicted to recorded fluorescence raises the per-neuron correlation
from $\rho \approx 0.06$ to $0.4$--$0.55$ while leaving heading
accurately decoded. Connectome and task fix \emph{that} the circuit
integrates; the recorded activity is needed to constrain \emph{how}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In the larval zebrafish, head-direction information is carried by a
population of hundreds of GABAergic inhibitory neurons --- the \rpi{}
cells of rhombomere~1 of the anterior hindbrain --- whose
ring-attractor-like activity tracks the animal's heading, and whose
\rpi{} axons have been reconstructed at synaptic resolution
\citep{petrucco2023}. We take this circuit as a test case for a more
general question: \emph{how much computation can a circuit with a
measured connectome support?} Electron microscopy
now gives the wiring of such circuits synapse by synapse, and it is
tempting to read the mechanism straight off the diagram. But a
connectome fixes only which neurons \emph{can} influence which; the
dynamics, the integration time constants, and the way variables are
laid out across cells are not given by the graph.

We therefore build models whose recurrent connectivity is \emph{locked}
to the \emph{neuprint-fish2} reconstruction --- only the synaptic
magnitudes, the afferent input gains, and a readout are learned --- and
train the same fixed wiring to solve a family of self-motion
integration tasks: maintaining heading, accumulating forward distance,
integrating a 2-D path, and tracking a mismatch between the sensory and
the proprioceptive angular drive. Each task asks what the measured
circuit \emph{can} compute; comparing the trained solutions, and
matching them to the recorded ZAPBench whole-brain calcium, asks how
much of the implementation the data actually pin down. This inverts the
usual forward-modelling logic: rather than hand-designing a ring
attractor, we ask whether the real wiring supports one, which
alternative circuits it \emph{also} supports, and what further evidence
--- recorded activity, behaviour --- is needed to choose among them. The
central result, developed below and mirrored in a companion
central-complex study \citep{cx2026}, is a separation between an
\emph{identifiable computation} --- connectome plus task robustly yield
a heading integrator --- and a \emph{non-identifiable implementation}
--- the same input--output behaviour is realised by many
connectome-consistent circuits, with the recorded activity needed to
narrow them down.

\section{Anatomy, functions and connectome}
\label{sec:anatomy}

\paragraph{3-D anatomy.}
\Cref{fig:zhd_3d} shows the circuit cell families rendered in
3-D and colour-coded by the same seven-way functional partition used
in the circuit diagram: the recurrent ring substrate
(\emph{dIPN} $=$ \emph{IPNd*} $+$ \emph{IPNds*}), the \emph{IPN12}
pool, and the \emph{IPN-core} families \emph{IPN28/29/31--36}; the
three afferent populations that
the velocity gate routes the angular and translational drives through
(\emph{ARTR} $=$ \emph{RIPN01/02/03\_a/03\_b}, \emph{pt-IPN1}, and
\emph{motor\_efferent} $=$ \emph{RIPN11/12\_a/12\_c}); and a residual
``other'' category for sub-types not routed through the gate
(\emph{RIPN05/12\_b}). Spatially, the
\emph{dIPN} substrate forms the compact midline neuropil;
\emph{RIPN}-derived afferents descend from the habenula (a paired
diencephalic nucleus relaying limbic and modulatory signals to the
hindbrain) and \emph{pt-IPN}-derived afferents sweep in from the
lateral pretectum (a midbrain visual-processing region carrying optic-
flow and water-flow signals).

\paragraph{Functional circuit.}
\label{method:proprioception}
The angular (heading) drive reaches IPN through two complementary
pathways (\cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{d}): the \emph{sensory} ARTR
afferents and a \emph{motor-efference} copy via the motor-efferent
RIPN sub-types \emph{RIPN11}, \emph{RIPN12\_a}, \emph{RIPN12\_c}
($36+29$ cells per side, glutamatergic) --- which the \emph{fish2}
inventory ties to head direction, not forward swim --- carrying the
turn command's efference copy. The proprioception variant adds these
cells as a companion \emph{angular} gate target: $\omega$ now drives
both \emph{ARTR} and \emph{motor\_efferent} in parallel (each with its
own pair of learnable scalars), while $v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ routes only
to \emph{pt-IPN1}. This rotation/translation split of the afferent
partition (\cref{fig:zhd_3d}) mirrors the functional organisation
\citet{feierstein2023} report in the zebrafish hindbrain: applying
reduced-rank regression to whole-brain GCaMP6s imaging, they find that
behaviour-related population activity is low-dimensional --- captured by
two features --- and that the corresponding voxels segregate into a
vergent-movement/swimming (translational) cluster and two rotation
clusters that lie selectively in the left and right hemispheres.

\paragraph{Afferent cell types.}
The constituent afferent sub-types carry distinct, identifiable
signals. \emph{ARTR} --- the Anterior Rhombomere Turning Region --- is
a long-time-constant population that typically becomes active only
after the first turning event and then sustains its activity over a
relatively long window. It is built from two similar pairs.
\emph{RIPN01} and \emph{RIPN03\_a} share a connectivity pattern that
differs essentially only in their ring target: \emph{RIPN01} projects
to \emph{dIPN}, whereas \emph{RIPN03\_a} projects to \emph{dsIPN}
($=$\emph{IPNds*}), a small region just posterior to dIPN.
\emph{RIPN02} and \emph{RIPN03\_b} form a second, structurally similar
pair --- with the targets swapped (\emph{RIPN03\_b}$\to$\emph{dIPN},
\emph{RIPN02}$\to$\emph{dsIPN}) --- that reads out the \emph{hSAG}
pathway carrying horizontal rotational signals; these cells are not yet
reliably driven in the present data (their activity resembles noise),
but their connectivity places them with ARTR.
\emph{pt-IPN1} integrates optic-flow and water-flow signals and divides
into roughly four groups, each encoding a different direction.
The motor-efference cells split by efferent source: \emph{RIPN11}
receives \emph{ascending} motor efferents together with the \emph{hSAG}
pathway and is our leading candidate for moving the heading bump, while
\emph{RIPN12\_a} and \emph{RIPN12\_c} --- best treated as a single
group --- receive \emph{descending} motor efferents together with
\emph{hSAG}.

\paragraph{Connectome.}
\Cref{fig:zhd_connectome_summary} depicts the nominal $917$-cell
circuit, its binary support $\mathrm{supp}(W^{\mathrm{con}})$ and the
signed $W^{\mathrm{con}}$. The matrix is sparse (density $0.037$,
$30\,851$ non-zero edges out of $917^2 = 840\,889$); edge weights are
the \emph{fish2} synapse contact areas, spectrally normalised to
radius $0.9$. The recurrent substrate --- the \rpi{} cells
(\emph{IPNd*}, \emph{IPNds*}), the \emph{IPN12} pool, and the
\emph{IPN-core} families --- is taken GABAergic: its
outgoing weights are sign-flipped to inhibitory, giving an
all-inhibitory recurrent substrate ($E\!:\!I = 10\,406\!:\!20\,445$,
$33.7\%$ / $66.3\%$). \emph{RIPN*} and \emph{pt-IPN*} afferents have
no Dale annotation on the \emph{fish2} server and are kept positive
(excitatory).

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\textbf{Recurrent neural network.}\label{method:RNN}
The RNN is a discrete-time recurrent network for
$i\in\{1,\dots,N\}$ with $N=917$:
\begin{equation}
\tau\,\frac{d\hat h_i(t)}{dt}
  = -\hat h_i(t)
  + \sum_j \hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij}\,\sigma\!\big(\hat h_j(t)\big)
  + \sum_l \hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{il}\,u_l(t)
  + \hat b_i,
\label{eq:zhd_dynamics}
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ is the sigmoid, $\tau=0.1\,\mathrm{s}$, $\Delta t=0.01\,\mathrm{s}$.
The recurrent matrix is sign-locked to the connectome template
$W^{\mathrm{con}}\in\R^{N\times N}$ (built from the
\emph{neuprint-fish2} reconstruction; outgoing columns of GABAergic
HD-ring cells \emph{IPNd*}, \emph{IPNds*} are negated, and the whole
matrix is spectrally normalised to radius $0.9$): the trainable
per-edge parameter is the magnitude $\hat S$, and
\begin{equation}
\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij}
  = |\hat S_{ij}|\,\mathrm{sign}(W^{\mathrm{con}}_{ij}).
\label{eq:zhd_w_rec}
\end{equation}
\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Input gate on $\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}$: anatomically licensed afferent ports.}%
\label{method:input_extended}
$\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}\in\R^{N\times n_{\mathrm{in}}}$ has one row per
neuron ($N=917$) and one column per input channel
($n_{\mathrm{in}}=\dim u(t)$). At every step it injects the external
drive $\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}u(t)\in\R^{N}$ into the dynamics, added to
the recurrent term $\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}\sigma(\hat h)$ of
\cref{eq:zhd_dynamics}. The rule is that each input variable may touch
only the one afferent cell type that
\cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{c}--\textbf{d} assigns to it; the three
afferent types are the only entry points, and the ring cells
(\emph{IPNd*}, \emph{IPNds*}, \emph{IPN12}) never receive external
input.

Take the exteroceptive variant of \cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{c}, with the
four-channel drive
\begin{equation*}
u(t)=\bigl(\,\omega,\ v_{\mathrm{fwd}},\
   \cos\theta_0\!\cdot\!\delta_{t_0},\
   \sin\theta_0\!\cdot\!\delta_{t_0}\,\bigr)^{\!\top},\qquad
\omega\to\mathrm{ARTR},\quad v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\to\text{pt-IPN1}.
\end{equation*}
Grouping the $N$ rows by population, the signal injected into the
network at time $t$ --- the $+\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}u(t)$ term of
\cref{eq:zhd_dynamics} --- is the matrix--vector product
{\small
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}\,u(t)=
\bordermatrix{%
 & \omega & v_{\mathrm{fwd}} & \cos\theta_0 & \sin\theta_0 \cr
\mathrm{ARTR}_L   & -\softplus(\tilde v_\omega^{L}) & 0 & * & * \cr
\mathrm{ARTR}_R   & +\softplus(\tilde v_\omega^{R}) & 0 & * & * \cr
\text{pt-IPN1}_L  & 0 & v_{\mathrm{fwd}}^{L} & * & * \cr
\text{pt-IPN1}_R  & 0 & v_{\mathrm{fwd}}^{R} & * & * \cr
\text{ring}       & 0 & 0 & * & * \cr
\text{other aff.} & 0 & 0 & * & * \cr}
\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\[1pt] v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\\[1pt]
   \cos\theta_0\,\delta_{t_0}\\[1pt] \sin\theta_0\,\delta_{t_0}\end{pmatrix}
\\[6pt]
=\ \omega\,\mathbf{w}_\omega+v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\,\mathbf{w}_{v}
  +\delta_{t_0}\bigl(\cos\theta_0\,\mathbf{c}+\sin\theta_0\,\mathbf{s}\bigr)
  \ \in\R^{N},
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:zhd_swim_gate}
\end{equation}
}
where ``$*$'' marks a free (unconstrained) entry and
$\mathbf{w}_\omega,\mathbf{w}_{v},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{s}\in\R^{N}$ are
the four columns of $\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}$. The three column kinds are:
the \emph{sign-locked turn column} $\mathbf{w}_\omega$
($\omega\!\to\!$ARTR), nonzero only on ARTR with its left half forced
inhibitory and right half excitatory via $\mp\softplus$ --- the two
scalars $\tilde v_\omega^{L},\tilde v_\omega^{R}$ are the only learned
parameters, and the lock makes left/right turns drive the ring peak in
opposite directions (a same-sign gate would integrate heading equally
well but drive both hemispheres in phase, unlike the data); the
\emph{unlocked vigour column} $\mathbf{w}_{v}$
($v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\!\to\!$pt-IPN1), non-directional, keeping the pt-IPN1
restriction but dropping the lock (one free scalar per side, either
sign); and the \emph{free cue columns} $\mathbf{c},\mathbf{s}$
($\cos\theta_0,\sin\theta_0$), dense and fully free over all $N$ rows,
which $\delta_{t_0}$ switches on only at the first frame $t_0$ to plant
the starting heading bump into the ring before they fall silent.
The proprioception variant of \cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{d} adds
a fifth channel,
\begin{equation*}
u(t)=\bigl(\,\omega,\ v_{\mathrm{fwd}},\ \omega_{\mathrm{proprio}},\
   \cos\theta_0\!\cdot\!\delta_{t_0},\
   \sin\theta_0\!\cdot\!\delta_{t_0}\,\bigr)^{\!\top},\qquad
\omega_{\mathrm{proprio}}\to\mathrm{motor\_efferent},
\end{equation*}
i.e.\ a \emph{second} sign-locked angular column routed to
motor\_efferent (RIPN11 + RIPN12\_a + RIPN12\_c, which the \emph{fish2}
inventory ties to head direction, carrying the turn command's efference
copy), while $v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ still routes only to pt-IPN1. In the
first model version the two angular columns share a driver,
$\omega_{\mathrm{proprio}}(t)=\omega(t)$, each with its own pair of
scalars. The same rule scales to the full multi-modal driver of the
nine ZAPBench conditions (\cref{fig:zhd_allblocks}): every visual
channel (rotation/optic-flow velocity, whole-field luminance, L/R light
difference, random-dot motion) drives pt-IPN1, and every tail-EMG
self-motion channel drives RIPN, each gated by \cref{eq:zhd_swim_gate}.
Anatomy fixes \emph{which} port a modality uses, the sign-lock fixes its
L/R \emph{polarity}, and only the gain $\softplus(\tilde v_c^{L,R})$ is
free.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Output readout on $\hat W^{\mathrm{out}}$: task-conditioned columns.}%
\label{method:output_readout}
$\hat W^{\mathrm{out}}$ reads from the HD ring (\emph{IPNd*},
\emph{IPNds*}, \emph{IPN12 pool}) and never from the afferent
populations. Its column dimension $n_{\mathrm{out}}$ is set by the
active sub-task (\cref{fig:zhd_swim_task_modes}):
\begin{equation}
\hat y(t) =
\begin{cases}
(\cos\hat\theta,\sin\hat\theta)                 & n_{\mathrm{out}}=2 \ \ (\text{heading only}),\\
(\cos\hat\theta,\sin\hat\theta,\hat d)          & n_{\mathrm{out}}=3 \ \ (\text{heading}+\text{forward distance}),\\
(\cos\hat\theta,\sin\hat\theta,\hat x,\hat y)   & n_{\mathrm{out}}=4 \ \ (\text{2D path integration}),
\end{cases}
\label{eq:zhd_readout}
\end{equation}
with $\hat y = \hat W^{\mathrm{out}}\,\sigma\!\big(\hat h(t)\big)+\hat b^{\mathrm{out}}$,
$\hat\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\hat y_2,\hat y_1)$. The leaky-vs-cumulative choice
(\cref{eq:xi_leaky,eq:xy_pi}) changes only what $\hat d$ and
$(\hat x,\hat y)$ are supervised against, not the readout shape.
The learnable parameters of the model are
\begin{equation*}
\big\{\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij},\;
\{\tilde v_c^{L},\tilde v_c^{R}\}_c,\;
\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{:,\theta_0},\,\hat b,\,
\hat W^{\mathrm{out}},\,\hat b^{\mathrm{out}}\big\};
\end{equation*}
the connectome template $W^{\mathrm{con}}$, the afferent
subpopulation membership, the neuron-type assignments, $\tau$ and
$\Delta t$ are given.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Graph neural network model.}\label{method:GNN}
The GNN model replaces the dense recurrent operator
$\sum_j \hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij}\,\sigma(\hat h_j)$ of
\cref{eq:zhd_dynamics} with a message-passing graph neural network
(Gilmer \emph{et al.}\ 2017), and --- crucially --- does \emph{not}
hard-code either the leak, the firing-rate nonlinearity, or the
synaptic activation function: the linear leak $-\hat h_i/\tau$, the
sigmoid $\sigma$, and the per-edge message that combines a
presynaptic activity with its postsynaptic effect must all be
discovered from the input--output map inside the learned drift
$f_\theta$ and the learned signalling function $g_\phi$. Beyond
removing this functional bias, the GNN also admits an explicit form
of neuronal heterogeneity through a per-neuron learnable embedding
$\mathbf a_i \in \R^d$ that enters both $f_\theta$ and $g_\phi$:
same-type neurons share their architectural role but can specialise
via their embedding, so a single pair of MLPs covers the $34$ fish2
cell types of \cref{sec:anatomy} and all $N = 917$ instance-level
distinctions without any further per-type parameter tying; we use
$d = 4$ as the default. Nodes correspond to neurons and edges to
the non-zero entries of the zebrafish HD connectome template
$W^{\mathrm{con}}$, and the subthreshold activity evolves as
\begin{equation}
\tau\,\frac{d\hat h_i(t)}{dt}
  = f_\theta\!\Big(\hat h_i(t),\,\mathbf a_i,\,
       \sum_{j \in \mathcal N_i}
       \hat W_{ij}\,g_\phi\!\big(\hat h_j(t),\mathbf a_j\big)\Big)
  + \sum_l \hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{il}\,u_l(t),
\label{eq:zhd_gnn_dynamics}
\end{equation}
with the same readout (\cref{eq:zhd_readout}), swim-gated encoder
(\cref{eq:zhd_swim_gate}), and dynamics constants ($\tau,\Delta t$)
as the RNN. The neighbour set is fixed by the connectome topology,
$\mathcal N_i = \{j : W^{\mathrm{con}}_{ij} \neq 0\}$. The node
update $f_\theta$ and edge message $g_\phi$ are three-layer
perceptrons (width 64, ReLU, linear output). $g_\phi$ maps the
presynaptic state $\hat h_j$ to a per-edge message conditioned on
$\mathbf a_j$ and weighted by the per-edge gain $\hat W_{ij}$;
$f_\theta$ then processes the postsynaptic state $\hat h_i$, the
embedding $\mathbf a_i$, and the aggregated synaptic drive. We
constrain $g_\phi$ to be monotonically increasing in $\hat h_j$ via
the prior $\partial g_\phi / \partial \hat h \ge 0$ (see
\cref{eq:zhd_gnn_loss}); combined with the sign-locked $\hat W_{ij}$
(\cref{eq:zhd_gnn_w}) this restores Dale's law at the per-edge level
without introducing a $\pm$-sign degeneracy. The leak, the
firing-rate non-linearity, and any departure from a linear synapse
must emerge from $(f_\theta, g_\phi, \mathbf a)$ themselves; the
monotonicity priors below provide the minimum inductive bias for
$f_\theta, g_\phi$ to behave like a neuron (postsynaptic drive
increases with net excitatory input). The per-edge weight is
sign-locked to the connectome as in the RNN,
\begin{equation}
\hat W_{ij}
  = |W_{ij}|\,\mathrm{sign}(W^{\mathrm{con}}_{ij}),
\label{eq:zhd_gnn_w}
\end{equation}
where $W_{ij} \in \R$ is the trainable per-edge magnitude.
In the zebrafish circuit $\mathrm{sign}(W^{\mathrm{con}}_{ij})$ is
negative for every edge whose presynaptic neuron is in the dIPN
(\emph{IPNd*} or \emph{IPNds*}) and positive for every edge from
\emph{RIPN*} or \emph{pt-IPN*}; the GNN therefore inherits the
inhibitory dIPN directly from the sign pattern. The learnable parameters are
\begin{equation*}
\big\{\theta,\,\phi,\,\mathbf a,\,\hat{W}_{ij},\;
\{\tilde v_c^{L},\tilde v_c^{R}\}_c,\;
\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{:,\theta_0},\,\hat b,\,
\hat W^{\mathrm{out}},\,\hat b^{\mathrm{out}}\big\};
\end{equation*}
the connectome template $W^{\mathrm{con}}$, the afferent
subpopulation membership, the neuron-type assignments, $\tau$ and
$\Delta t$ are given.

As with the RNN, heading recovery from cos/sin readouts is
fundamentally non-unique: many distinct $(\theta,\phi,\mathbf a,W)$
configurations produce identical readouts. We constrain the solution
space with the same connectome-prior regularisers as the RNN
($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}$ from
\cref{eq:zhd_cos,eq:zhd_nf}, computed on the per-edge gain surface
$\hat W_{ij}$ with floor threshold $\kappa$), together with sparsity
and monotonicity priors on the MLPs:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L} \;=\;&
  \tfrac{1}{BT}\sum_{b,t}\|\hat y_{b,t} - y_{b,t}\|_2^2
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{cos}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{nf}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}} \nonumber\\
& +\;\lambda_0\|\theta\|_1 + \lambda_1\|\phi\|_1
   + \lambda_2\|\theta\| + \lambda_3\|\phi\|
   \;+\;\gamma_0\|W\|_1 + \gamma_1\|W\|_2 \nonumber\\
& +\;\mu_0\!\left\|
       \relu\!\left(+\tfrac{\partial f_\theta(\hat h,\mathbf a,m)}
                          {\partial \hat h}\right)\right\|
   +\;\mu_1\!\left\|
       \relu\!\left(-\tfrac{\partial f_\theta(\hat h,\mathbf a,m)}
                          {\partial m}\right)\right\| \nonumber\\
& +\;\mu_2\!\left\|
       \relu\!\left(-\tfrac{\partial g_\phi(\hat h,\mathbf a)}
                          {\partial \hat h}\right)\right\|
   +\;\mu_3\,\big\|g_\phi(h_\star,\mathbf a)-h_\star\big\|,
\label{eq:zhd_gnn_loss}
\end{align}
where $m = \sum_{j \in \mathcal N_i}\hat W_{ij}\,
g_\phi(\hat h_j,\mathbf a_j)$ is the aggregated synaptic input. The
$\ell_1$ penalties $\lambda_0,\lambda_1$ on the MLP weights bias
$f_\theta, g_\phi$ toward sparser, simpler functions; the $\ell_2$
penalties $\lambda_2,\lambda_3$ prevent weight explosion. The
leak-monotonicity term $\mu_0$ penalises \emph{positive} slopes of
$f_\theta$ in the postsynaptic state $\hat h$, encoding the prior
that the drift contains a leak
($\partial f_\theta / \partial \hat h \le 0$); $\mu_1$ penalises
negative slopes of $f_\theta$ in the aggregated input $m$, encoding
the prior that postsynaptic drive increases with net excitatory
input. Together, $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ force the discovered drift to be
decreasing in self-voltage and increasing in synaptic input --- the
two qualitative signatures of \cref{eq:zhd_dynamics}. The term $\mu_2$
enforces the analogous monotonicity prior on $g_\phi$ in the
presynaptic voltage. The normalisation term $\mu_3$ anchors $g_\phi$
at a reference subthreshold value $h_\star$, removing the
multiplicative degeneracy between $g_\phi$ and $\hat W$ in
\cref{eq:zhd_gnn_dynamics}. Hyperparameters
$\lambda_{\{0,1,2,3\}},\,\gamma_{\{0,1\}},\,\mu_{\{0,1,2,3\}}$ and the
MLP widths/depths are tuned with an agentic optimisation procedure,
with $r_{\mathrm{roll},1k}$ --- the Pearson correlation between the decoded
and true heading over a $1\,000$-step deterministic swim-impulse rollout
--- as the primary score.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Swim-integration task.}%
\label{method:swim_task_modes}\label{method:xi_leaky}
The on-disk swim-integration dataset is a \emph{single superset} from
which several sub-tasks are projected at training time. Swim events are
sampled at a Poisson rate $r_{\mathrm{swim}}=0.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ with
event duration $\Delta t_{\mathrm{swim}}=0.3\,$s, partitioned into four
behavioural categories with fractions $L:R:F:B=0.3:0.3:0.2:0.2$. Left
and right events drive an angular signed phase impulse, forward and
backward events drive a signed forward-velocity impulse. The input is a
$4$-channel stream $u\in\R^{B\times T\times 4}=[\,\omega,\,v_{\mathrm{fwd}},\,
\cos\theta_0\!\cdot\!\delta_{t,0},\,\sin\theta_0\!\cdot\!\delta_{t,0}\,]$:
left/right events drive $\omega$, forward/backward events drive
$v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$, and the initial heading $(\cos\theta_0,\sin\theta_0)$
enters as a single-frame impulse at $t=0$ (position needs no such cue, as it
starts at the origin). The target column layout depends on the sub-task: the
\emph{scalar} recipe stores $y=[\,\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,d\,]$ with
$\theta(t)=\theta_0+\int_0^t\omega\,ds$ and $d(t)=\int_0^t v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\,ds$
($d(0)=0$), while the \emph{2D path-integration} recipe stores
$y=[\,\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,x,\,y\,]$, projecting $v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ through
the current heading and integrating each spatial axis independently
(\cref{eq:xy_pi}). The 2D recipe is the \emph{true} path-integration target: it
couples translation to heading, so the network must maintain $\theta$
internally to predict $(x,y)$.

The default scalar $d$ and the default 2D position $(x, y)$ are both
\emph{cumulative} integrators of their respective drives. With a non-zero
mean forward-swim drive these targets grow linearly with the
curriculum horizon, so the loss scales with $T$ and cross-snapshot
comparisons become awkward. A finite time constant $\tau>0$ replaces
the cumulative sum with the leaky recurrence
\begin{equation}
d(t+\Delta t) = \left(1-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_d}\right)d(t)
    + v_{\mathrm{fwd}}(t)\,\Delta t,
\qquad d(0)=0,
\label{eq:xi_leaky}
\end{equation}
equivalent to $\dot d = -d/\tau_d + v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$. For the
2D-position target the same leaky law is applied independently to each
axis,
\begin{equation}
\frac{dx}{dt} = -\frac{x}{\tau_p} + v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\cos\theta,
\qquad
\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{y}{\tau_p} + v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\sin\theta,
\qquad x(0)=y(0)=0,
\label{eq:xy_pi}
\end{equation}
which keeps the trajectory bounded near the recent egocentric
displacement. Biologically this is the natural choice: real neural
integrators leak, and what the fish actually encodes is a
low-pass-filtered estimate of \emph{recent} forward swim vigour and
recent displacement, not absolute long-horizon position. The
production runs use $\tau_d=\tau_p=0.5\,$s, with the
cumulative-integrator recipes preserved as separate variants so the loss
and metric comparisons stay clean.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Proprioceptive-gain mismatch task.}\label{method:mismatch}
The proprioception circuit (\cref{method:proprioception}) lets us pose a
sharper question than redundant sensory$+$efference integration: can the
circuit track a \emph{discrepancy} between its two angular streams? We
drive ARTR with the observed angular velocity $\omega$ and
motor\_efferent with a \emph{gain-modulated} proprioceptive copy
$\omega_{\mathrm{proprio}}(t) = g(t)\,\omega(t)$, where the gain $g(t)$
is a piecewise-constant process drawn uniformly in $[0,1.5]$ that steps
to a new value every ${\approx}2\,$s --- a discrete proprioceptive-gain
regime switch, in the spirit of the closed-loop gain manipulations of
the ZAPBench \textsc{gain} block. The supervised third readout column is
no longer a forward distance but the integrated mismatch
\begin{equation}
m(t) = \int_0^t \big(\omega(s) - \omega_{\mathrm{proprio}}(s)\big)\,ds
     = \int_0^t \big(1 - g(s)\big)\,\omega(s)\,ds,
\label{eq:zhd_mismatch}
\end{equation}
the accumulated sensory-vs-proprioceptive heading discrepancy --- zero
when proprioception is veridical ($g\equiv1$) and growing whenever the
two streams disagree. The input is the $5$-channel proprioception layout
$[\omega, v_{\mathrm{fwd}}, \omega_{\mathrm{proprio}},
\cos\theta_0\delta_{t_0}, \sin\theta_0\delta_{t_0}]$ ($n_{\mathrm{in}}=5$)
and the readout is $\hat y=(\cos\hat\theta,\sin\hat\theta,\hat m)$
($n_{\mathrm{out}}=3$ --- the same column count as the
heading$+$distance recipe of \cref{eq:zhd_readout}, but with the third
column supervised against $m$). Recovering $m$ forces the recurrent
circuit to maintain a running difference of its two anatomically
distinct afferent streams rather than echo either one.

\paragraph{Training.}

\bigskip
Training optimises a per-frame MSE
on the heading readout with two connectome-prior regularisers and
an $\ell_1$ sparsity prior on the recurrent magnitudes,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
= \tfrac{1}{BT}\sum_{b,t} \|\hat y_{b,t} - y_{b,t}\|_2^2
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{cos}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{nf}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}
+ \lambda_{1}\,\|\hat{W}^{rec}_{ij} \|_1,
\label{eq:zhd_loss}
\end{equation}
with block losses computed over type-pair masks
$\mathcal{B}_p \subset \{1,\dots,N\}^2$ (one block per ordered
pre/post neuron-type pair $p \in \mathcal{P}$),
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}
  &= \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}}
     \!\left[1 - \frac{\langle \hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{\mathcal{B}_p},\,
         W^{\mathrm{con}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}\rangle}
       {\|\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}\|\,
        \|W^{\mathrm{con}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}\|}\right],
\label{eq:zhd_cos}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}
  &= \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}}
     \relu\!\big(\kappa -
       \overline{|\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}|}\big)^2.
\label{eq:zhd_nf}
\end{align}
The cosine-distance term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}$ anchors each
block to the connectome template; the norm-floor term
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}$ prevents any type-pair block from
collapsing to zero by penalising the squared shortfall of the block
mean absolute weight $\overline{|\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}|}$
below a fixed floor $\kappa$ (typical value $\kappa = 0.05$). Because
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}$ is scale-invariant,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}$ is required to prevent a degenerate
solution in which the cosine prior is satisfied by an arbitrarily
small recurrent magnitude. The type-pair vocabulary $\mathcal{P}$ is
the cartesian product of the 34 fish2 cell types identified in
\cref{sec:anatomy}, restricted to pairs with at least one non-zero
entry in $W^{\mathrm{con}}$.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Calcium observation loss.}\label{method:obs}
The heading loss of \cref{eq:zhd_loss} constrains only the
two-dimensional readout $\hat y(t)$; the $N$-dimensional recurrent state
$\hat h(t)$ is left unobserved, so the task alone does not pin the
internal dynamics. To constrain the implementation we add an optional
term that matches the model's predicted calcium to \emph{recorded}
activity. A GCaMP indicator turns the model's per-neuron activity into a
fluorescence trace by causal convolution with a unit-area
difference-of-exponentials kernel,
\begin{equation}
k_{\mathrm{GCaMP}}(t) \;\propto\; e^{-t/\tau_d} - e^{-t/\tau_r},
  \quad t \ge 0,
\qquad
\hat c_i(t) \;=\; \big(k_{\mathrm{GCaMP}} * \hat h_i\big)(t),
\label{eq:zhd_gcamp}
\end{equation}
with nominal cytosolic GCaMP7f rise/decay constants
$(\tau_r,\tau_d) = (0.15,\,1.20)\,\mathrm{s}$ \citep{dana2019}. ZAPBench
imaged the slower nuclear \emph{H2B-GCaMP7f}, so \cref{eq:zhd_gcamp} if
anything \emph{under}-states the low-pass smearing of the real observable.
The kernel is normalised to unit area so the transform
preserves the mean, the convolution is evaluated on the model grid
$\Delta t$, and it is differentiable, so gradients flow from the
fluorescence back into $\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}$, the afferent gate and
$\hat b$. Let $\mathcal{O}$ index the $|\mathcal{O}| = 481$ HD-circuit
neurons matched to a recorded ROI (below) and let $c^{\star}_i(t)$ be the
recorded $\Delta F/F$ of neuron $i$. Because the indicator gain and
baseline are not identifiable, the term is made invariant to a per-neuron
affine rescaling by $z$-scoring both signals over time before comparison,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}}
  = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{O}|\,B_c T}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}}\sum_{b,t}
    \big(z[\hat c_i]_{b,t} - z[c^{\star}_i]_{b,t}\big)^2 ,
\qquad
z[x]_{b,t} = \frac{x_{b,t} - \mu_{b,i}}{\sigma_{b,i}},
\label{eq:zhd_obs}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{b,i},\sigma_{b,i}$ are the per-trial, per-neuron mean and
standard deviation over time. For unit-variance signals
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}} = 2\,(1-\rho)$ with $\rho$ the Pearson
correlation, so the term rewards temporal \emph{shape} agreement rather
than absolute scale. The full objective adds it to \cref{eq:zhd_loss}
with weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}$,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathcal{L}
  + \lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}}$;
setting $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$ (or supplying no calcium trials)
recovers the task-only model exactly.

\paragraph{Mutual-information estimators.}\label{method:MI}
We use mutual information to quantify how strongly each unit's
firing rate $r_i(t) = \sigma(\hat h_i(t))$ tracks the heading
$\theta(t)$ (or, where stated, the angular velocity $\omega(t)$ or
the swim-onset trace $s(t)$) over a held-out rollout. For a single
neuron we wrap $\theta$ to $(-\pi, \pi]$ via
$\theta \leftarrow \arg(e^{i\theta})$, discretise the wrapped angle
into $n_\theta = 32$ equal-width angular bins and $r$ into $n_r = 20$
equal-width bins, then compute the plug-in estimator in bits,
\begin{equation}
\hat I(r_i;\theta) = \sum_{b,c} \hat p(b,c)\,\log_2
\frac{\hat p(b,c)}{\hat p(b)\,\hat p(c)},
\label{eq:zhd_mi_plugin}
\end{equation}
where $\hat p$ is the empirical joint over the
$(\theta\!\!-\!\!\mathrm{bin}, r\!\!-\!\!\mathrm{bin})$ histogram. The
plug-in construction is used so that the per-neuron and
per-cell-type quantities of
\cref{sec:results_four_classes} are
comparable across cell types and sub-populations. For a group of $K$ neurons we
use the cross-validated lower bound
\begin{equation}
\hat I_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mathbf r_{1:K};\theta) =
H(\theta) - \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal D_{\mathrm{te}}|}
\sum_{(t,\theta)\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{te}}}
-\log_2 q_\psi(\theta_b\mid \mathbf r(t)),
\label{eq:zhd_mi_joint_lb}
\end{equation}
where $q_\psi$ is an $L_2$-regularised multinomial logistic
regression with input $\mathbf r \in \mathbb R^K$ and output the
$n_\theta$ heading bins, trained on a stratified 5-fold split.

\paragraph{Decoder choice shapes the recurrent representation.}
\label{sec:results_decoder}

In \cref{sec:results_evolution} the heading was read out by a linear map
onto $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and decoded with $\operatorname{atan2}$.
The readout is not a neutral observer: because the recurrent weights are
connectome-locked and only re-scaled, the \emph{form} of the decoder is
one of the few degrees of freedom left to the optimiser, and it can
shape the internal representation the network settles on. We therefore
re-train the same $917$-cell circuit on the same heading task with two
alternative heading decoders, holding the recurrent dynamics and the
afferent gate fixed: (i) a \emph{$64$-bin softmax} readout, in which the
heading is one of $64$ angular bins (a localist ring code) decoded by the
softmax-weighted circular mean ($n_{\mathrm{out}}=64$); and (ii) a
\emph{nonlinear MLP} readout replacing the linear map.

The $64$-bin decoder converges as well as the $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$
readout: heading is recovered across held-out trials and constant-$\omega$
sweeps, and the dIPN still carries a single travelling activity peak, now
read out directly as a moving band in bin space
(\cref{fig:zhd_evolution_kbins}, panel \textbf{h}). The nonlinear MLP
decoder also converges, but only once the optimisation is \emph{paced}.
Trained at equal rates, the flexible MLP readout collapses to the target
mean (chance) before the connectome-locked ring can form --- a
chicken-and-egg a linear readout avoids, because its optimum already
requires a structured ring. Letting the recurrent core lead and the
decoder follow (a higher, gently decaying recurrent learning rate against a
slower readout rate) removes the imbalance, and the MLP then recovers
heading as cleanly as the other two readouts (per-trial $r_\theta=0.999$,
median circular RMSE $1.3^\circ$; \cref{fig:zhd_evolution_mlpdec}). A small
capacity sweep confirms this is not tuned to one architecture: five
variants spanning hidden width $8$--$128$ and depth $2$--$4$ all converge
($r_\theta\ge0.994$, RMSE $1.3$--$3.9^\circ$). The takeaway is
methodological: with the recurrent substrate fixed to the connectome, the
decoder is an active modelling choice that interacts with the dynamics ---
a too-flexible readout can out-run the circuit and must be paced so the
integrator forms first --- but none of the three readouts (cos/sin,
localist bin, or nonlinear MLP) changes the underlying solution, and the
ARTR diagnostic confirms the recurrent dynamics are qualitatively unchanged
(\cref{fig:zhd_artr_oscillation_kbins}).


\paragraph{Proprioceptive-gain mismatch on the dual-afferent circuit.}
\label{sec:results_mismatch}

The proprioception circuit (\cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{d}) routes the
angular drive through two anatomically distinct afferent streams --- the
sensory ARTR copy $\omega$ and a gain-modulated motor-efference copy
$\omega_{\mathrm{proprio}}=g(t)\,\omega$
(\cref{method:mismatch}; \cref{fig:zhd_swim_task_modes}\textbf{d}).
Trained to report the integrated mismatch
$m=\int(\omega-\omega_{\mathrm{proprio}})\,dt$ alongside heading, the same
sign-locked $917$-cell RNN recovers both. Unlike the heading, which is
encoded as the unit vector $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and decoded by
$\operatorname{atan2}$, the mismatch $m$ is an unbounded cumulative
quantity and is supervised directly as a scalar --- a plain regression
head trained under the same per-frame MSE. Across held-out swim trials the
decoded heading and the decoded mismatch each track ground truth at
per-trial Pearson $r\approx0.999$, and the mismatch trace follows the
piecewise-constant gain regime-switches frame by frame
(\cref{fig:zhd_evolution_mismatch}, panel \textbf{g} and row \textbf{j}).
The circuit therefore does not merely echo one afferent stream: it
maintains a running \emph{difference} of the two, accumulating the
sensory-vs-proprioceptive heading discrepancy --- a quantity that is zero
only when proprioception is veridical. This is the connectome-locked
analogue of a forward-model error signal, and it shows the dual-afferent
wiring can support gain-mismatch detection, not just redundant
integration.


\subsection{Trained heading + distance integrator}
\label{sec:results_variants}

We do not know a priori which integration the larval circuit actually
performs --- whether it maintains a scalar forward distance or a full
2-D position, and whether either is accumulated losslessly or leaked.
Rather than commit, we train all four combinations ---
(distance vs.\ 2-D path) $\times$ (cumulative vs.\ leaky) --- on
the same connectome-locked circuit and compare. This brackets the
biologically plausible options: the cumulative target is the idealised
path integrator, the leaky target the bounded \emph{recent-displacement}
estimate a real leaky neural integrator would hold.

The same $917$-cell circuit and sign-locked RNN trains successfully

and, where applicable, the leaky-integrator time constant

\paragraph{Leaky vs.\ cumulative: bounded steady-state radius.}
\label{res:leaky_vs_cumulative}
The cumulative and leaky variants of the swim-integration task share the
same recurrent circuit, the same input drive, and the same heading head;
they differ only in the shape of the supervised distance and position
target. The cumulative target is the indefinite integral of the
corresponding velocity, which grows linearly under a sustained constant
drive. The leaky target replaces the integrator with the first-order leak
of \cref{eq:xi_leaky,eq:xy_pi}, whose steady-state response to a constant
drive is bounded.

For the 2-D position target under constant $v_{\mathrm{ext}}$ and constant
angular velocity ($\theta(t) = \omega t$), writing $z = x + iy$ turns
\cref{eq:xy_pi} into the linear ODE
$\dot z = -z/\tau_p + v_{\mathrm{ext}}\,e^{i\omega t}$. Substituting the
steady-state ansatz $z(t) = A\,e^{i(\omega t + \varphi)}$ gives
$A\,e^{i\varphi} = \tau_p\,v_{\mathrm{ext}}/(1 + i\,\tau_p\,\omega)$, so
\begin{equation}
R \;\equiv\; |A| \;=\; \frac{\tau_p\,v_{\mathrm{ext}}}
                            {\sqrt{1 + (\tau_p\,\omega)^{2}}},
\qquad
\varphi \;=\; -\arctan(\tau_p\,\omega).
\label{eq:leaky_radius}
\end{equation}
\Cref{eq:leaky_radius} is the response magnitude and phase of a
first-order low-pass of cutoff $1/\tau_p$ driven by a complex sinusoid at
frequency $\omega$: the DC gain is $\tau_p\,v_{\mathrm{ext}}$, the gain
rolls off by $1/\sqrt{1+(\tau_p\,\omega)^2}$ at frequency $\omega$, and
$\varphi$ is the lag of the decoded position behind the instantaneous
heading.

Two limits of $R$ are useful. For $\tau_p\,\omega \ll 1$ the radius is
$R \approx \tau_p\,v_{\mathrm{ext}}$, the asymptote of the leaky
integrator in the no-rotation limit. For $\tau_p\,\omega \gg 1$ the
radius is $R \approx v_{\mathrm{ext}}/\omega$, the kinematic radius of
the circle traced by a forward-moving rotating object; $\tau_p$ drops
out entirely in this regime.

In \cref{fig:zhd_swim_task_modes}\textbf{(c)} the cumulative path drifts
unboundedly from the origin under the constant $(\omega,
v_{\mathrm{ext}})$ drive, while the leaky path stays inside the disk of
radius $R$ given by \cref{eq:leaky_radius}. For the scalar-distance
variant (\cref{eq:xi_leaky}) the same argument gives
$\tau_d\,v_{\mathrm{ext}}$ as the steady-state forward distance under
constant $v_{\mathrm{ext}}$. The practical consequence for training is
that the supervised target stays $\mathcal{O}(1)$ across the curriculum
horizon, so the per-frame loss carries no $T$-dependent scale and the
distance / position heads are directly comparable across curriculum
stages. The cumulative variants are retained as the unbounded reference
reported alongside the leaky variants in
\cref{tab:zhd_subtask_metrics}.

\paragraph{Message-passing GNN: same circuit, same heads.}
\label{res:gnn_subtasks}
The same connectome-locked circuit also trains under the message-passing
GNN of \cref{method:GNN}, which replaces the dense recurrent operator
$\sum_j\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij}\sigma(\hat h_j)$ with per-edge $g_\phi$
and per-neuron $f_\theta$ MLPs over a learnable per-neuron embedding
$\mathbf a_i$. The leak, the firing-rate nonlinearity, and the synaptic
activation are no longer hard-coded; they must emerge from
$(f_\theta, g_\phi, \mathbf a)$. We train the GNN on three of the
swim-integration sub-tasks --- heading-only, 2-D path with the
cumulative target, and 2-D path with the leaky target --- on the same
on-disk dataset, the same anatomy-routed afferent gate, and the same
heading and $(x, y)$ readout heads as the sign-locked RNN. The bottom
block of \cref{tab:zhd_subtask_metrics} reports the per-trial Pearson
$r$. On heading the GNN reaches $r_\theta = 0.997 \pm 0.008$, within
the SD of the RNN baseline ($0.998 \pm 0.023$). On the harder 2-D path
the GNN matches the RNN on the leaky target
($\bar r_{xy} = 0.945 \pm 0.155$ vs.\ $0.945 \pm 0.152$) and slightly
exceeds it on the cumulative target
($0.837 \pm 0.209$ vs.\ $0.760 \pm 0.268$). Lifting the hard-coded
leak, firing-rate nonlinearity, and synaptic activation does not
collapse the task solution: the circuit reaches the same
per-frame accuracy under a substantially less constrained dynamical
parameterisation, consistent with the integration being a property of
the connectome and the afferent partition rather than of a particular
choice of single-cell dynamics.
\Cref{fig:zhd_gnn_dashboard} shows the trained heading-only GNN in the
same panel layout as \cref{fig:zhd_evolution_artr_pt1}, with an extra
row reporting the three quantities the GNN adds over the sign-locked
RNN: the per-neuron embedding and the two learned functions (the
per-edge signalling function and the node update) that replace the
fixed leak and firing-rate nonlinearity. Trained on the joint targets the
GNN also reproduces the recurrent code: its pooled mutual information shows
the same distributed structure as the RNN --- single neurons carry little,
the whole pool reaches the ceiling --- across all four joint-target
variants (\cref{fig:zhd_mi_partition_gnn}, the companion of
\cref{fig:zhd_mi_partition}).


\paragraph{Summary: per-frame accuracy cannot pick the integrator --- biology must.}
All five runs converge to a working integrator on the same connectome,
and per-frame accuracy is saturated for every one of them
($r_\theta$, $r_d$, $\bar r_{xy}$ all $\ge 0.99$ on held-out trials,
\cref{tab:zhd_subtask_metrics}); the constant-$\omega$ integration gain
also stays unit-slope whether or not forward-distance or 2-D position is
decoded alongside heading. Per-frame task accuracy therefore
\emph{cannot} select among the four target recipes --- the choice is a
question for biology, not for the loss. Two axes are open, and the
evidence points differently on each.

\emph{Leaky vs.\ cumulative is not a binary --- every biological
integrator leaks.} A perfectly cumulative integrator is an
idealisation: it is a network-level property requiring a marginally
stable line- (or plane-) attractor with \emph{exactly} unit feedback,
which is not physically realisable \citep{seung1996,khona2022}. Any
real recurrent circuit has a finite time constant set by its recurrent
gain, so ``cumulative'' is the idealised limit and ``leaky'' the
realistic one; the only question is \emph{how} leaky --- the value of
$\tau$. That leak is moreover empirically tunable: in the goldfish and
zebrafish oculomotor integrators the persistence time constant is
detuned across an order of magnitude and retuned by feedback
\citep{major2004,goncalves2014}. Our own ``cumulative'' runs make the
point teeth-first: under a sustained constant drive the trained
cumulative distance head extrapolates with a gain far below unity
(slope ratio $\approx 0.09$--$0.003$), i.e.\ the connectome-locked
recurrence cannot hold a true line attractor and \emph{the cumulative
target is already being approximated by a leaky solution} --- the
substrate wants to leak. Behaviour points the same way: a larval fish
navigating over seconds needs to know how far and where it has gone
\emph{recently}, not lifetime odometry, and that recent-displacement
estimate is exactly a bounded, leaky integral. The one exception is
heading: the \rpi{} bump persists for tens of seconds without drift,
the signature of a ring attractor held marginally stable by its
\emph{topology} rather than by fine tuning
\citep{kim2017,chaudhuri2019}. The soundest configuration is therefore
\emph{asymmetric} --- effectively non-leaky heading alongside
finite-$\tau$ translation --- and the two channels should not share a
single leak constant.

\emph{2-D vs.\ distance-only remains open.} The 2-D path target is the
more rigorous task: only it couples translation to the internally
maintained heading --- the defining feature of true path integration ---
whereas distance-only is heading-blind odometry. A vector-summation
path integrator of exactly this kind has been demonstrated in the fly
central complex \citep{lyu2022}, and the zebrafish connectome plainly
supports the heading$\leftrightarrow$translation coupling: the leaky 2-D
model integrates the path at $\bar r_{xy}=0.95$
(\cref{tab:zhd_subtask_metrics}), which is only possible if the circuit
maintains $\theta$ internally to project translation. That the spatial
estimate is then carried as a \emph{distributed}, low-per-neuron-MI code
(\cref{sec:results_four_classes}) is a fact about its layout, not its
presence.
Allocentric place coding does exist in the larval zebrafish
\emph{telencephalon} \citep{yang2024}, but no allocentric position
(place- or grid-like) code is established in the anterior-hindbrain /
IPN circuit we model, so the model's 2-D success states what the
\emph{wiring can compute}, not that this circuit holds a 2-D position
estimate. Distance-only is the conservative claim; 2-D the
ambitious-but-unevidenced one.

Both axes make at the task level the point the rest of the paper makes
at the weight level: the input--output map is degenerate. Per-frame
accuracy fixes neither the leak constants nor the dimensionality of the
integrated variable; biology must, and the present evidence says only
\emph{leaky translation alongside near-lossless heading, with the 2-D
question open}.

\subsection{Angular / displacement partition of the recurrent pool}
\label{sec:results_four_classes}

\paragraph{Overview.}
\emph{The integrated heading lives in individual neurons, the integrated
position lives only in the population.}
For four models trained to report heading together with a displacement
target (a $1$-D distance or a $2$-D path, each with and without leak), we
measured for every recurrent neuron how much it carries about the heading
and about the displacement, and separately read out the same two
quantities from the whole pool at once (\cref{fig:zhd_mi_partition}).
Three findings stand out.

\emph{(i) Heading is encoded redundantly at both scales.} Many single cells
are strongly heading-tuned, and any sizeable group of neurons decodes
heading near the information ceiling. It is the robust, over-represented
variable.

\emph{(ii) The $2$-D position is encoded only collectively --- it emerges
through the network.} This is the central point. In the $2$-D models
\emph{no single recurrent neuron carries the position}: per-neuron
information about it is essentially zero (the faint single-neuron cloud in
the right-hand columns). Yet the \emph{population} decodes the $2$-D
position near the ceiling (the large whole-pool dot), exactly matching the
network's high path-integration accuracy ($\bar r_{xy}=0.95$). The apparent paradox
--- ``the model integrates the path yet has no position-tuned cells'' ---
is not a contradiction but the definition of a \emph{distributed code}: the
spatial estimate is a collective property of the recurrent circuit that no
neuron expresses on its own. Asking single neurons returns nothing; asking
the population returns the answer.

\emph{(iii) The split is computational, not anatomical, and the code is
saturated.} Heading- and position-carrying activity is drawn from all four
GABAergic families with only a graded bias, and is \emph{not} inherited
from the cleanly separated afferents --- the recurrent dynamics reorganise
the angular and translational drives into one mixed, distributed code.
That code is strongly redundant: group information grows only
\emph{logarithmically} with neuron number, so the $700$-cell pool already
sits at saturation and the marginal neuron adds almost nothing.

The rest of this section substantiates each point.

\paragraph{Single neurons versus the population.}
We restrict the analysis to the recurrent GABAergic substrate
(\emph{IPNd*}, \emph{IPNds*}, the \emph{IPN12} pool and the
\emph{IPN-core} families; $n=700$ of the $917$ cells; the afferents are
excluded, their drive-locked MI being trivially high). For each recurrent
neuron we measure the mutual information its activity carries about heading
and about displacement (circular MI for $\theta$, plug-in MI for $d$; for
the 2-D models $d$ is the radial position $\lvert(x,y)\rvert$, not the
trajectory arc length, which a leaky integrator discards). We then read the
same two quantities out of progressively larger \emph{groups} --- each
fish2 cell type, each anatomical family, and the whole pool --- as a
cross-validated decoder lower bound (for the 2-D models, of the joint
$(x,y)$ position the rollout scores). \Cref{fig:zhd_mi_partition} plots all
of these in one heading-versus-displacement plane, dot area growing with
group size (single cells smallest, the whole pool largest).

The picture is unambiguous: \emph{information lives in the population, not
in single cells.} The single-neuron cloud sits low --- in the 2-D models
essentially no cell carries more than a fraction of a bit about position
--- yet the whole-pool dot (black) climbs to the ceiling, decoding heading
at $\approx\!3.7$ bits in every model and position at up to $\approx\!3.4$
bits. This resolves what looks like a contradiction with the rollout
metrics: the leaky 2-D model has no position-tuned neurons yet integrates
the path at $\bar r_{xy}=0.95$ (\cref{tab:zhd_subtask_metrics}). There is no
conflict --- the spatial estimate is a \emph{distributed} population code
that the tuned readout reads but a per-neuron (or single-scalar) estimator
cannot.

\emph{More task, more information.} From task to task the pooled code
climbs as the target grows richer, and the joint heading-plus-2-D-path code
is the richest of the four: in the cumulative 2-D model
(\cref{fig:zhd_mi_partition}\textbf{c}) the whole pool reaches the ceiling
on \emph{both} axes at once ($\approx\!3.6$ bits heading, $\approx\!3.4$
bits position) --- the best trade-off of the set. Heading alone saturates
already at the smallest groups, so it is the more robustly and redundantly
encoded of the two variables.

\paragraph{The code is distributed, not anatomical.}
Colouring the dots by anatomical family shows the mixing is not read off
the wiring. No family occupies its own part of the plane: heading- and
position-carrying activity is drawn from all four GABAergic families
(\emph{dIPN}, \emph{dsIPN}, \emph{IPN12}, \emph{IPN-core}) with only a
graded bias, even though the \emph{afferent} stage separates the two drives
cleanly --- the angular ARTR copy and the exteroceptive pt-IPN1 translation
copy enter through anatomically distinct ports (\cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{c}).
Which recurrent neuron ends up heading- or position-tuned is therefore set
by the connectome-locked recurrent computation, which reorganises the
cleanly separated afferent drives into one mixed, distributed code --- not
read off its afferent-input identity.

\paragraph{Does the MI add up?}
The dot sizes of \cref{fig:zhd_mi_partition} make the redundancy
quantitative: pooled information climbs only \emph{logarithmically} with
group size (a cross-validated decoder lower bound, \cref{eq:zhd_mi_joint_lb}).
Fitting
$I_{\mathrm{group}}\approx a + b\,\log_{10} N$ across all $112$ groups
(four families and $24$ cell types, pooled over the four models) gives
slope $b\approx0.80$ bits/decade for heading and $0.93$ for displacement;
the log form fits markedly better than linear, square-root, or simple
saturating alternatives ($R^2=0.41$ vs.\ $0.22$, $0.31$, and $<0$ for
heading, pooled --- and tighter still within each model, $R^2$ up to
$0.88$). The slope is the quantitative statement of diminishing returns: a
tenfold increase in neuron number adds only $\approx\!0.8$ bits, so the
cost of \emph{doubling} the code escalates with the operating point ---
from $\sim\!18\times$ more neurons to go from $1$ to $2$ bits, to
$\sim\!10^{4}\times$ to double the circuit's actual $\approx\!3.7$-bit
heading code, a population increase no real circuit could supply and one
the entropy ceiling forbids in any case. The connectome therefore operates
deep in the saturated, redundant regime: the integrated heading and
position codes are essentially complete in the $700$-cell recurrent pool,
and the marginal recurrent neuron carries almost no new information.

\subsection{Task accuracy does not imply realistic activity}
\label{sec:results_task_vs_activity}
Solving the task is not the same as matching the brain. Driven by the
recorded stimulus, the task-only circuit decodes heading well yet produces
calcium quite unlike the recording --- the core degeneracy that a
connectome plus a task loss leaves unresolved, since many circuits realise
the same computation with different activity. A calcium-observation loss
that pulls predicted activity toward the recording partially closes this
gap, and the fish's own behaviour bounds how far any such model can reach.
We develop these observation-matching experiments --- the task-only
baseline, the behavioural covariates, the observation-loss sweep, and the
proprioceptive 2-D variant under calcium supervision --- in
\cref{app:calcium}; the discussion and outlook below build on them.


\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Taken together, the four results establish a single claim: a circuit whose
recurrent wiring is \emph{fixed} to the measured larval connectome --- only
the edge magnitudes, the afferent gate, and the readout are free --- can be
trained to perform the navigational computations the inferior--peduncular
region is thought to support, and the resulting model is informative about
\emph{how} the wiring does so.

First, the sign-locked circuit learns a near-unit-gain heading integrator
on the nominal connectome (\cref{sec:results_evolution}): a single
travelling activity peak tracks heading across held-out swims and
constant-drive sweeps. That integrator is a property of the recurrent
substrate, not of the observer reading it out --- a localist ring, a
linear, and a nonlinear readout all recover the same dynamics, the only
caveat being that an over-flexible readout must be paced so the integrator
forms first (\cref{sec:results_decoder}). The same circuit, given two
anatomically distinct angular afferents, maintains a running
sensory-vs-proprioceptive \emph{difference} rather than echoing either
stream --- a connectome-locked forward-model error signal
(\cref{sec:results_mismatch}).

Second, the circuit integrates heading together with translation, but
per-frame accuracy alone cannot decide \emph{which} translation the larval
brain performs: leaky and cumulative integrators, and 1-D distance versus
2-D path, all fit the swim statistics about equally well, so the choice
must be made on biological grounds rather than on fit quality
(\cref{sec:results_variants}). Third, the recurrent code that supports this
is distributed and saturated: heading is encoded redundantly down to single
cells, the 2-D position only collectively --- no neuron carries it, yet the
population decodes it near the information ceiling --- and the split between
the two is set by the recurrent computation, not read off the cleanly
separated afferents, with pooled information growing only logarithmically in
neuron number (\cref{sec:results_four_classes}). Fourth, solving the task is
not the same as reproducing the brain: a connectome plus a task loss leaves
a genuine activity degeneracy that a calcium-observation loss only partially
closes, with the fish's own unmodelled behaviour setting the ceiling
(\cref{sec:results_task_vs_activity}).

\paragraph{Link map to the outlook.} Each result opens a directly
matching question, developed in \cref{sec:outlook_future}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The connectome-locked integrator (\cref{sec:results_evolution})
    raises whether the wiring is \emph{necessary} or merely sufficient
    (\hyperref[out:necessary]{is the connectome necessary?}) and within
    what inhibitory window it holds
    (\hyperref[out:ei]{what E/I ratio supports joint heading\,$+$\,translation?}).
  \item The path-integration models (\cref{sec:results_variants}) set up
    the sparsity floor below which integration fails
    (\hyperref[out:sparsity]{minimum connectivity for path integration}).
  \item The distributed, saturated code (\cref{sec:results_four_classes})
    leads to whether coding is specialised or mixed
    (\hyperref[out:mixed]{specialised or mixed-selectivity coding?}) and to
    the geometry of the population estimate itself
    (\hyperref[out:manifold]{what \emph{is} the distributed translation code?}).
  \item The dual-afferent mismatch signal (\cref{sec:results_mismatch})
    motivates separating the two translation drives by test-time ablation
    (\hyperref[out:proprio]{exteroceptive vs proprioceptive translation}).
  \item The activity gap (\cref{sec:results_task_vs_activity}) asks how much
    identifiability calcium supervision buys
    (\hyperref[out:degeneracy]{how much does calcium supervision reduce solution degeneracy?})
    and whether a learned drive can close what remains
    (\hyperref[out:inr]{can the recording be recovered by injecting a learned drive?}).
  \item The inherited, drive-locked oscillation
    (\cref{sec:results_task_vs_activity}, \cref{app:calcium}) points to its
    origin and causal role
    (\hyperref[out:oscillation]{how and why are the fast oscillations generated?}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook: which circuit properties are necessary, sufficient,
and biologically meaningful?}
\label{sec:outlook_future}

The results leave open the harder question of \emph{what about this
particular network} is actually doing the work. Nine directions follow
directly from the experiments already in this paper and together would
convert the present existence proof into a structural account of the
computation.

\paragraph{Is the connectome necessary?}\label{out:necessary} Re-training the same task
family on random networks matched for size, density, and E/I ratio
--- and on systematically rewired versions of the measured
connectome --- isolates how much of the observed performance comes
from the wiring as opposed to the network's capacity. The expected
gradient (intact $\to$ degree-preserved rewiring $\to$
Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi at matched density) directly speaks to whether
the larval brain's connectivity is computationally efficient or
merely sufficient. The size-, density- and weight-scale-matched
random null connectomes for this control --- sweeping the
recurrent-pool inhibitory fraction --- are constructed in
\cref{app:er_null} (\cref{fig:zhd_er_connectomes}).

\paragraph{What E/I ratio supports joint heading + translation?}\label{out:ei}
The recurrent pool is predominantly inhibitory, and the v1 vs v2
connectome variants flip the IPN12 sign while keeping every other
edge fixed. Sweeping a softer version of that switch --- a controlled
inhibitory fraction at constant density --- and re-fitting the joint
swim-integration task would identify the E/I window inside which
both heading and forward distance remain decodable, and how close
the measured ratio sits to its centre.

\paragraph{Minimum connectivity for path integration.}\label{out:sparsity} An edge-pruning
sweep against the trained both / position-2D models, scoring the
task error as edges are removed in order of magnitude, gives the
sparsity floor below which integration fails. The same sweep
distinguishes a smoothly degrading regime (graceful) from a
brittle one (cliff at a critical sparsity), and identifies the
small core of edges that carries the computation.

\paragraph{Specialised or mixed-selectivity coding?}\label{out:mixed} The
angular/displacement partition of \cref{sec:results_four_classes} is
a coarse readout of how the trained recurrent pool divides the work.
A finer per-neuron mutual-information analysis on $\hat\theta$,
$\hat d$, and their interaction would say whether the circuit
converges on disjoint heading and distance subpopulations or relies
on neurons that jointly encode both --- and whether mixed selectivity
emerges only when the task complexity demands it.

\paragraph{How much does calcium supervision reduce solution
degeneracy?}\label{out:degeneracy} The observation loss already shifts each model toward a
small neighbourhood of recordings-consistent solutions
(\cref{sec:results_obs_sweep}). Training $K$ independent seeds with
$\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$ and again with $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}
> 0$ and comparing the spread of $W^{\mathrm{rec}}$, $W^{\mathrm{in}}$,
$W^{\mathrm{out}}$, and the resulting per-cell tuning would quantify
how much identifiability the observation loss adds beyond the
connectome support and the task loss alone.

\paragraph{Exteroceptive vs proprioceptive translation.}\label{out:proprio} The
proprioceptive variant (\cref{method:proprioception}) routes
$v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ through motor-efferent cells in parallel with the
exteroceptive (pt-IPN1) pathway. Ablating each pathway in turn at
test time --- on the same trained circuit --- decomposes the
translation drive into the two physiologically distinct sources and
predicts which calcium signatures should disappear under selective
silencing of either limb, providing a directly testable bridge to
in-vivo perturbation experiments.

\paragraph{What \emph{is} the distributed translation code?}\label{out:manifold}
\Cref{sec:results_four_classes} showed that the recurrent population
reads out 2-D position near the information ceiling while no single
neuron does --- a distributed code. What that code actually \emph{is}
remains open: whether the position lives on a low-dimensional manifold of
population activity embedded in the $700$-cell space, how the heading and
translation axes are oriented relative to one another there, and whether
the same subspace is reused or rotated as the task moves from $1$-D
distance to a $2$-D path. Locating the position subspace by dimensionality
reduction, measuring its geometry, and testing whether a low-rank readout
suffices would turn ``distributed'' from a statement about per-neuron
information into an explicit account of how the circuit holds a place
estimate.

\paragraph{How and why are the fast oscillations generated --- and do they
drive heading?}\label{out:oscillation} The recorded ARTR carries a fast hemispheric alternation
that the connectome-locked model reproduces only while driven and loses in
darkness (\cref{fig:zhd_artr_oscillation,fig:zhd_artr_dark}): in the model
the oscillation is \emph{inherited}, not intrinsic. Whether the biological
rhythm is generated locally --- by recurrent left$\leftrightarrow$right
inhibition plus a slow adaptation variable --- or inherited from upstream,
and, crucially, whether it \emph{drives} the heading estimate or merely
rides on it, is unresolved. Adding the two missing ingredients to the
connectome-locked circuit and asking whether a self-sustained oscillation
then emerges, and whether suppressing it degrades integration, would test
both its origin and its causal role.

\paragraph{Can the recording be recovered by injecting a learned drive?}\label{out:inr}
The calcium reconstruction is bounded from above by signals the model never
sees --- chiefly the fish's own behaviour (\cref{sec:results_unmodeled}).
Rather than hand-craft those inputs, one could \emph{learn} the missing
drive as a continuous implicit neural representation (an INR mapping time,
or behavioural state, to an injected proprioceptive / efference signal)
trained jointly with the observation loss so that the connectome-locked
circuit reproduces the recorded activity. If a low-dimensional learned
drive closes the remaining gap, it would both name the behavioural variable
the circuit is tracking and give a generative model of the recording; if it
does not, the residual marks structure the connectome-locked dynamics
genuinely cannot produce.

\section{Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi null-connectome control}
\label{app:er_null}

To make the necessity question of \cref{sec:outlook_future} concrete we
construct a family of random null connectomes against which the same
heading task can be re-fitted. Each null is a \emph{full-network} random
rewiring of the measured $917$-cell circuit, matched to it on three
statistics: the number of nodes ($N=917$), the edge count (and hence
density: $30{,}851$ edges, density $0.037$), and the weight scale --- each
random edge magnitude is drawn independently from the empirical
distribution of measured synaptic weights. Crucially the \emph{node
identities} are inherited unchanged from the measured circuit: the
cell-type assignment, the afferent gate targets (the populations the
angular and translational drives are injected into) and the recurrent
ring ordering used by the decoder are all preserved, so the random
circuits remain valid drop-in substrates for the same trained model ---
only the adjacency support, its sign pattern, and the edge magnitudes are
randomised.

The four nulls differ along a single controlled axis: the
\emph{recurrent-pool inhibitory fraction}. Following Dale's law each cell
is given a single output polarity; the afferent populations are kept
excitatory (as in the measured circuit), while a controlled fraction
$p_{\mathrm{inh}}$ of the recurrent pool is made inhibitory. The four
settings $p_{\mathrm{inh}}\in\{0.4,0.6,0.8,1.0\}$ span overall inhibitory
edge fractions of $30.7\%$, $46.0\%$, $61.4\%$ and $76.8\%$; the last,
$p_{\mathrm{inh}}=1.0$, reproduces the measured all-inhibitory recurrent
pool and so doubles as the size-, density- and E/I-matched necessity
control, while the lower settings additionally probe the inhibitory-fraction
window of \cref{sec:outlook_future}. The measured circuit's $66.3\%$
inhibitory fraction falls between the two most-inhibitory nulls.

\Cref{fig:zhd_er_connectomes} renders the measured connectome and the four
nulls in the same $z$-scored, partition-sorted signed-matrix format as
\cref{fig:zhd_connectome_summary}. The measured matrix is visibly
block-structured --- the functional partition organises the recurrent
inhibitory blocks and the excitatory afferent columns --- whereas the
nulls are structureless by construction, with the inhibitory (blue)
fraction growing across the sweep and the afferent columns remaining
excitatory throughout. Re-fitting the heading task on each null and
comparing the recovered integration accuracy against the intact circuit
is the experiment of \cref{sec:outlook_future}; the matched random
substrates and the training configuration are prepared here.

\section{ZAPBench training data}\label{method:zapbench}
The recorded activity is the publicly released ZAPBench whole-brain
light-sheet calcium recording of a larval zebrafish, registered to the
same \emph{fish2} EM volume as the connectome (\cref{sec:anatomy}): the
$\Delta F/F$ trace of every segmented cell is taken from the ZAPBench
release and each connectome \emph{bodyId} is matched to its functional
ROI through the EM\,$\leftrightarrow$\,functional registration. Of the
$839$ neurons in the IPN12 circuit, $481$ carry a matched recording ---
$300$ ring-pool cells (\emph{IPNd}/\emph{IPNds}/\emph{IPN12}) and $181$
afferents (\emph{RIPN}, \emph{pt-IPN}). \Cref{fig:zhd_allblocks} lays out
the whole $\sim\!2\,\mathrm{h}$ recording: the nine task blocks, the fish's
own swim behaviour, the recorded activity of the three velocity-gate
afferent classes (ARTR, pt-IPN1, motor\_efferent), and the ring pool ---
the same $481$ matched neurons used throughout. The observation loss is
taken over all $481$ matched cells; the real-vs-model kinographs display only
the $300$ ring-pool subset. (These matched-cell counts are for the
$839$-cell \emph{IPN12 precursor} on which the calcium observation-loss
experiments of \cref{sec:results_obs_sweep} were run --- the circuit
\emph{before} the IPN-core families were added to form the nominal
$917$-cell circuit of \cref{sec:anatomy}. The task-only calcium baseline
of \cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline} has since been re-run on the full
$917$-cell circuit, where $279$ ring-pool cells carry a matched
recording; the task-integration results of \cref{sec:results} all use
the $917$-cell circuit.) We supervise on the ``Rotations'' block, in which a
$45^\circ/\mathrm{s}$ drifting grating rotates the animal's virtual
heading: the true heading is read from the recorded stimulus channel and
differentiated to the angular-velocity drive $s(t)$, so the \emph{model}
is driven by the \emph{same} stimulus that produced the recording. The
block spans ${\sim}600\,\mathrm{s}$ ($656$ imaging frames at the
$1.09\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $0.915\,\mathrm{s}$ imaging rate). Because the model
runs at $\Delta t = 0.01\,\mathrm{s}$, the recorded $\Delta F/F$ is
linearly interpolated up to the model grid (edge-padded), and each neuron
is $z$-scored \emph{globally} over the whole block --- not per window ---
so the target keeps a single, trial-independent scale. The block is then
sliced into $1000$-frame ($10\,\mathrm{s}$) trials drawn from disjoint
train/test time spans, in the same format as the synthetic
swim-integration trials: channel~0 is the recorded drive $s(t)$ and the
first frame carries the $(\cos\theta_0,\sin\theta_0)$ heading cue, with no
warm-up. During training each task minibatch is augmented with $B_c$ of
these calcium trials in a single forward pass; the heading MSE of
\cref{eq:zhd_loss} is taken over the combined batch (the rotation trials
also carry a true heading), while $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is
evaluated on the calcium trials only.

\section{Reconstructing the recorded calcium activity}
\label{app:calcium}

\subsection{Task ability does not imply realistic activity}
\label{sec:results_obs_baseline}

The results so far establish that the connectome-constrained network learns
to integrate heading accurately, and that the solution is robust to any
single-type lesion. Accuracy on the task, however, says nothing about
whether the network's \emph{internal activity} matches the brain. We test
this directly by driving the trained task-only model
(\emph{zebrafish\_hd\_si\_ipn\_917\_v1}, the nominal $917$-cell circuit,
trained on the rotation task \emph{only}) with the recorded ZAPBench
``Rotations'' stimulus (\cref{method:zapbench}), converting its
rolled-out activity to GCaMP7f calcium (\cref{eq:zhd_gcamp}), and
comparing it to the recorded $\Delta F/F$ of the $279$ matched ring-pool
neurons in the same rastermap row order (\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}).

The model is an excellent head-direction predictor --- its decoded heading
tracks the $45^\circ/\mathrm{s}$ rotation closely
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}, bottom panel) --- yet its population activity is
plainly \emph{not} the recorded one: the model's learned kinograph is a
clean, stimulus-locked set of bands, whereas the recorded $\Delta F/F$
above it is irregular, bursty and drift-laden. This
is the degeneracy at the heart of the paper, now read out on real data:
\emph{many circuits realise the same head-direction computation, and hence
the same task ability, while producing different activity}. Task
supervision constrains only the two-dimensional readout
(\cref{eq:zhd_readout}); the $N$-dimensional state it leaves unobserved is
exactly where implementations diverge.

A natural objection is that the mismatch is merely cosmetic: perhaps the
recorded activity still encodes heading, just in a form our eye does not
read off the kinograph. We tested this directly by training a decoder ---
a small neural network --- to predict the instantaneous heading
$(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ from the recorded per-frame $\Delta F/F$ of the
ring-pool neurons (fit on the first $70\%$ of the rotation block,
evaluated on the held-out tail). The result was \emph{not} conclusive: the
decoded heading does not track the true heading
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_control}; recorded-$\Delta F/F$ decode at circular
$r=0.14$), in sharp contrast to the model's own readout, which
recovers heading almost perfectly from its latent state
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}, bottom panel). The implication is that \emph{the
instantaneous head-direction signal is hard to read, frame by frame, from
the observed calcium activity}.

How much of this failure is the \emph{observable} rather than the brain? We
settle the confound with a control: run the \emph{identical} decoder on the
trained model pushed through the \emph{same} observation pipeline --- its
rolled-out activity convolved with the GCaMP kernel, sampled at the
$1.09\,\mathrm{Hz}$ imaging rate and restricted to the same $300$ cells
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_control}). The model provably encodes heading: its latent
readout decodes the heading at circular $r = 0.97$. Through the degraded
observable the \emph{same} circuit decodes at only $r = 0.73$ --- the
observation pipeline alone (GCaMP low-pass of the fast $45^\circ/\mathrm{s}$
ring activity, $\tau_d = 1.2\,\mathrm{s}$, \cref{eq:zhd_gcamp}; $1.09\,\mathrm{Hz}$
aliasing of an $8\,\mathrm{s}$ revolution; only $300$ of $839$ cells seen)
removes a substantial fraction of the per-frame legibility. It does
\emph{not}, however, reach the real-data level ($r = 0.14$): a circuit that
encoded heading as cleanly as the model would still be decodable at
$r \approx 0.73$ under the recording's observation, so the observable is a
major but \emph{not} the sole cause. The residual gap reflects additional
real-world degradation --- recording noise, behavioural variability of the
animal relative to the grating, and the idealised, stimulus-locked model
ring activity being crisper than the brain's. Either way heading is carried in the
population's \emph{temporal dynamics}, not in any single calcium frame ---
which is why the observation loss matches the time-course of each neuron's
trace (\cref{eq:zhd_obs}, a per-neuron shape loss) rather than asking the
activity to decode heading frame-by-frame.

This motivates the calcium observation loss (\cref{eq:zhd_obs}): by adding
a term that pulls the model's predicted calcium toward the recorded
$\Delta F/F$, we aim to \emph{partially break} this degeneracy ---
selecting, among the task-equivalent circuits, those whose activity is
also consistent with the measured brain. Runs that train with the
observation loss are reported in \cref{sec:results_obs_sweep};
\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline} is the no-observation-loss
($\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$) baseline against which they are compared.

\subsection{Signals not yet accounted for: the fish's own behaviour}
\label{sec:results_unmodeled}

The task-only model's calcium is essentially uncorrelated with the
recording (\cref{sec:results_obs_baseline}; \cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}),
and the ceiling on what any model can recover here is set in part by
what the model is \emph{allowed to see}. The only time-varying input
to the network is the angular-velocity drive $s(t)$, which for the
Rotations block is an essentially periodic $\pm 45^\circ/\mathrm{s}$
square wave. A deterministic circuit driven by a periodic input can
only produce (after transients) a periodic output, so \emph{any}
non-periodic structure in the recorded $\Delta F/F$ is, by construction,
outside the model's reach --- and the recorded ring activity is
conspicuously non-periodic across the $600\,\mathrm{s}$ block even
though the stimulus repeats.

\Cref{fig:zhd_unmodeled} lays the recording's covariates side by side
over the rotation block to make the missing signals explicit. Above
the recorded $\Delta F/F$ we plot, from the raw ZAPBench ephys, the
fish's own swimming behaviour --- the quantity the model never
receives. The two bottom traces are the left and right tail-muscle
EMG channels (the independent motor readout); the larval-zebrafish
swim decode summarises them as a \emph{forward} drive (bilateral
envelope) and a \emph{turning} signal (left/right asymmetry). These
behave very differently with respect to the stimulus. Turning is
largely stimulus-locked --- it correlates with the drive at
$\rho \approx 0.76$, the expected optomotor following of the grating
--- and so a stimulus-driven model can in principle capture it.
Forward drive, by contrast, is a slow, non-periodic latent: its
lag-one autocorrelation is $\approx 0.97$ and it is essentially
uncorrelated with the stimulus magnitude ($|\rho| \lesssim 0.05$). It
drifts on the tens-of-seconds-to-minutes timescale that dominates the
recurrent pool's irregular activity, yet nothing in $s(t)$ carries
it.\footnote{The forward/turning traces are derived from the two
tail-EMG electrodes by the standard ZAPBench swim-EMG pipeline
(windowed-variance envelope, swim-bout detection, per-bout weighting);
we verified they are genuinely those channels by recovering
\emph{forward} from the bilateral EMG sum ($\rho \approx 0.57$) and
\emph{turning} from the left$-$right difference ($\rho \approx 0.42$).
The per-frame RMS shown here is a coarse envelope of the same
channels.}

Three classes of signal are therefore present in the recording but
absent from the current model. (i) \emph{Self-generated motor drive}
--- the forward/turning behaviour above, which is internally generated
and not a deterministic function of the visual stimulus; the forward
component in particular injects exactly the slow, aperiodic variation
the model cannot synthesise. (ii) \emph{Slow physiological and state
drift} --- bleaching, arousal, and adaptation accumulate over the
block, which is why per-trial $z$-scoring is necessary in
\cref{eq:zhd_obs} to remove this component rather than fit it.
(iii) \emph{Unmodelled sensory channels} --- the recording also
carries the raw visual-velocity and other stimulus channels at full
temporal resolution, of which the model receives only the distilled
heading drive. None of these are failures of identifiability: they
are inputs the network is simply not given. With this in mind we now
turn the observation loss on (\cref{sec:results_obs_sweep}) and see
how far it can close the gap.

\paragraph{Overall: do the results agree with the introduction?}
The introductory picture is a ring-attractor head-direction circuit ---
hundreds of GABAergic \rpi{} cells in the anterior hindbrain that carry a
single sinusoidal activity peak travelling around the functional ring,
persisting for tens of seconds, and tracking the swum heading, supported
by the reciprocal dIPN connectivity the EM reconstruction reveals. Our
trained, connectome-locked circuit reproduces
the computational core of this picture: it integrates heading at
near-unit gain and carries a single travelling activity peak on the dIPN
ring (\cref{sec:results_evolution}), realised on the measured fish2
wiring with the magnitudes sitting on the signed connectome support. In
that sense the results agree with the introduction --- the ring-attractor
account is realisable on the real connectome. They also sharpen its open
questions in two ways the introduction did not anticipate. First, the
implementation is \emph{degenerate}: many connectome-consistent circuits
integrate equally well, the preferred-heading map is not anatomically
organised, and the recurrent pool mixes heading and translation rather
than segregating them by cell type (\cref{sec:results_four_classes}) ---
so anatomy fixes \emph{that} the circuit integrates, not \emph{how} the
code is laid out. Second, the introductory ring activity is only
\emph{partly} matched on the recorded data: the task-only model's calcium
is nearly uncorrelated with the recording until the observation loss is
added (\cref{sec:results_obs_sweep}), and the recorded ARTR carries an
intrinsic dark-state rhythm that the command-driven model lacks
(\cref{sec:results_artr}). The agreement is therefore at the level of the
\emph{computation} the introduction describes, while the
\emph{implementation} and the \emph{full recorded dynamics} remain
under-determined --- the gap the rest of the paper sets out to measure.

\subsection{Calcium-observation loss partially closes the activity gap}
\label{sec:results_obs_sweep}

We now turn the observation loss on. Using the sign-locked RNN of
\cref{method:RNN} (the same recurrent dynamics used throughout
Section 4) we trained the $839$-cell \emph{IPN12 precursor} circuit
(\cref{method:zapbench}; the connectome before the IPN-core families
were added to reach the nominal $917$-cell circuit) on the
swim-integration task (dataset~A) while augmenting each minibatch with
$B_c$ recorded ``Rotations'' trials (dataset~B) and matching predicted
to recorded calcium with the $z$-scored shape loss of \cref{eq:zhd_obs},
sweeping the observation weight
$\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} \in \{0,\,0.1,\,0.5,\,1,\,5\}$
($\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}=0$ is the task-only control). Each trained model is then driven by the
held-out recorded stimulus and its rolled-out activity rendered to
GCaMP7f calcium exactly as for the baseline (\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}),
and scored against the recorded $\Delta F/F$ over a continuous
$600\,\mathrm{s}$ rollout (\cref{tab:zhd_obs_sweep},
\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction}). The figure of merit is the per-neuron
temporal $z$-MSE of \cref{eq:zhd_obs}, equal to $2(1-\rho)$ with $\rho$
the predicted-vs-recorded Pearson correlation, reported separately for
the $300$ ring-pool cells and the $181$ afferents.

The loss does what it is designed to. With $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$
the task-only model's calcium is essentially uncorrelated with the
recording ($z$-MSE $\approx 1.88$ for both pools, $\rho \approx 0.06$):
the network integrates heading perfectly yet its activity is unrelated to
the brain's, exactly the degeneracy of \cref{sec:results_obs_baseline}.
Turning the loss on collapses this error immediately --- already at the
smallest weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0.1$ the ring-pool $z$-MSE
drops to $1.25$ ($\rho \approx 0.38$) and the afferent pool to $0.92$
($\rho \approx 0.54$) --- and larger weights buy little further reduction
($z$-MSE plateaus at $1.1$--$1.3$ for the ring activity peak and $0.90$--$0.98$ for the
afferents across $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} \in [0.1, 5]$). Most of the
benefit is therefore captured by the mere presence of the term rather
than its magnitude. The afferent (\emph{RIPN}/\emph{pt-IPN}) pool is
reconstructed markedly better than the recurrent pool (afferent
$\rho \approx 0.54$ and SSIM up to $0.44$, vs.\ ring-pool $\rho \approx 0.40$
and SSIM $\approx 0.18$), consistent with the afferents carrying the
stimulus-locked part of the recording while the ring activity peak is the irregular,
drift-laden activity that is hardest to match.

The fit is partial, not complete, and trades against the task at large
weights. Even at its best the ring-pool correlation plateaus near
$\rho \approx 0.4$: the loss pulls the predicted activity toward the
recording but does not reproduce it, so the degeneracy of
\cref{sec:results_obs_baseline} is \emph{partially}, not fully, broken
--- the expected outcome for a single per-neuron shape loss applied to
$481$ of the $839$ cells. Pushing $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}$ higher does not
help and begins to hurt: the structural SSIM peaks at the smallest weight
($\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0.1$) and declines thereafter, and the
decoded-heading panels (\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction}, bottom)
develop more frequent slips as the observation term competes with the
heading MSE. An intermediate weight
($\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} \approx 0.1$--$0.5$) is thus the operating point
that best balances calcium realism against task accuracy.

\paragraph{Task generalisation across the gcamp family.}
The observation-loss recipe is task-agnostic --- the per-neuron
$z$-scored shape loss does not refer to heading, distance, or any
other behavioural variable --- so the same training procedure can
be swapped onto the other swim-integration sub-tasks of
\cref{sec:results_variants}. We train two such companions of
\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction}, both supervised against the
recorded \emph{Rotations} block of dataset~B: (i) the joint
heading-plus-distance task (\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction_both},
the ``selfmotion\_both'' four-input variant) and (ii) the
translation-only task
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction_translation},
``selfmotion\_translation''). For the four-input model the
continuous test-time rollout is now driven jointly by the recorded
$\omega(t)$ and the recorded swim-forward envelope
$v_{\mathrm{fwd}}(t)$ (read from the calcium dataset's
swim-forward channel, the same channel the trainer feeds into the
calcium-batch trials) --- i.e.\ the model sees at test exactly the
four channels it was trained on. For the translation-only model
the rollout is driven by $v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ alone. The pattern is
consistent across these two variants: the afferent pool
reconstructs at $\rho \approx 0.4$ (SSIM $0.39$--$0.48$), the
recurrent ring-pool at $\rho \approx 0.2$--$0.3$ (SSIM
$0.20$--$0.25$), and the per-frame task readouts (heading and/or
distance) remain accurately decoded in every case; the observation
loss does not collapse onto the task heads. The Turning block of
dataset~B is excluded from this comparison because its recorded
stimulus is stored as discrete L/R direction codes (no angular
velocity in the dataset's first channel), which leaves the input
gate driving the trained heading-only model with zeros and
therefore cannot supervise the heading-driven activity meaningfully.

\subsection{Proprioceptive 2-D path integration under calcium supervision}
\label{sec:results_propriocep}

The proprioception-extended circuit of \cref{method:proprioception}
(\cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{d}) routes the forward-swim drive
$v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ to IPN through two anatomically distinct ports in
parallel --- the exteroceptive \emph{pt-IPN1} channel and the
motor-efferent \emph{RIPN11/12\_a/12\_c} channel --- and is trained on
the full 2-D path-integration task ($n_{\mathrm{in}}=5$,
$n_{\mathrm{out}}=4$, target $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,x,y)$) \emph{with}
the calcium-observation loss on (dataset~B ``Rotations'',
$\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}=1$, $B_c=8$). This is the single circuit in
which the proprioceptive afferent split, the hardest (2-D path) target,
and the real-calcium supervision are all active at once.
The proprioceptive circuit differs from the task-only 2-D models
(\cref{fig:zhd_evolution_position2d,fig:zhd_evolution_position2d_leaky})
only in the second (proprioceptive) afferent port and the added
observation term. Heading is still recovered on held-out swim trials (per-trial
Pearson $r_\theta \approx 0.94$), and the 2-D-position head integrates
the path at a per-frame Euclidean RMSE of $\approx 0.38$ on natural
trials for the cumulative variant --- the calcium term and the extra
afferent port do not degrade the task.

Driving these same two checkpoints with the recorded ZAPBench Rotations
stimulus and rendering the rolled-out activity to GCaMP7f calcium
(\cref{eq:zhd_gcamp}) gives the reconstructions of
\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction_propriocep_position2d,fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction_propriocep_position2d_leaky}.
The layout follows \cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction_translation}, except
that the bottom panel shows the decoded 2-D \emph{trajectory}
$(\hat x,\hat y)$ against the cumulative path-integration reference, in
spatial coordinates, rather than the scalar forward-distance trace. As
elsewhere the afferent pool reconstructs markedly better than the
recurrent ring pool (cumulative: afferent $z$-MSE $=0.97$, SSIM $=0.43$
vs.\ ring-pool $z$-MSE $=1.12$, SSIM $=0.17$; leaky: afferent
$z$-MSE $=0.94$, SSIM $=0.59$ vs.\ ring-pool $z$-MSE $=1.04$,
SSIM $=0.20$), consistent with the afferents carrying the
stimulus-locked part of the recording. The decoded trajectory makes the
leaky-vs-cumulative contrast explicit on the continuous $600\,\mathrm{s}$
rollout: the cumulative decode drifts away from the origin while the
leaky decode stays bounded near it, the spatial analogue of the scalar
forward-distance trace in \cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction_translation}.

\subsection{ARTR: opponent L/R gate and the origin of the oscillation}
\label{sec:results_artr}

The angular afferent pool (ARTR $=$ \emph{RIPN01/02/03\_a/03\_b};
\cref{tab:zhd_mapping}) is the anterior rhombencephalic turning region of
\citet{dunn2016artr}: a slow ($\sim$10--20\,s) antiphase oscillation between
the left and right clusters that biases successive swim bouts.

\paragraph{Sign-locked opponent gate.}
\emph{Hypothesis:} the recorded ARTR L/R anti-correlation needs an opponent
gate the heading task does not by itself impose. \emph{Test:} train the
angular gate unconstrained versus locked to opposite polarity
($v_{c,L}=-\softplus(\tilde v_{c,L})\le 0$,
$v_{c,R}=+\softplus(\tilde v_{c,R})\ge 0$, magnitudes free). \emph{Result:} the
unconstrained gate converges to a same-sign solution --- it integrates heading
equally well but drives both hemispheres in phase; the lock restores opponency
at no cost to task accuracy. Non-directional channels (forward-swim vigour,
luminance) carry no lock.

\paragraph{Two timescales.}
\emph{Hypothesis:} ARTR carries a slow stimulus-locked rhythm plus a faster
component of unknown origin (inherited vs intrinsic). \emph{Test:} band-split
the L/R means at a $16$\,s period and compare L/R coherence
(\cref{fig:zhd_artr_timescales}). \emph{Result:} over the whole rotation block the recorded ARTR L/R
population means are antiphase ($r_{LR}=-0.55$) and the sign-locked model
is more sharply so ($r_{LR}=-0.93$). Band-splitting separates two
components. The slow band ($>16$\,s,
$\sim$60\,s) is the turn-block --- antiphase and $\omega$-locked in both data
($r_{LR}=-0.57$) and model ($-0.96$), hence inherited. The fast band
($<16$\,s, $\sim$8\,s) is the heading-bump carrier ($360/|\omega|$) in the
model and is L/R phase-locked ($|r_{LR}|=0.90$); in the recording it is
incoherent ($r_{LR}=+0.03$) and left-lateralised ($7$\,L vs $1$--$2$\,R cells)
where the model is bilateral. An inherited
carrier is necessarily L/R-locked, so the recorded fast band is a graded mix
--- inherited carrier plus a cell-specific component at or below the
imaging-SNR floor.

\paragraph{Decoder dependence of the modelled ARTR.}
Driving the $64$-bin-decoder model (\cref{sec:results_decoder}) with the
same recorded $\omega$ gives the companion kinograph of
\cref{fig:zhd_artr_timescales_kbins}. The modelled ARTR is again
antiphase and $\omega$-locked --- the recorded structure is reproduced
under either readout --- but the L/R opponency is somewhat weaker than
with the $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ readout (whole-trace $r_{LR}=-0.59$
vs.\ $-0.93$; slow band $-0.94$ vs.\ $-0.96$). The decoder therefore does
leave a measurable imprint on the recurrent representation, even though
the qualitative inherited-oscillation picture
(\cref{fig:zhd_artr_oscillation_kbins}) is unchanged.

\paragraph{Origin: inherited, not intrinsic.}
\emph{Hypothesis:} an intrinsic oscillator has a period set by its own time
constants --- invariant to $\omega$, sustained, and persistent without input;
an inherited one tracks the drive and stops with it. \emph{Test:} a
constant-$\omega$ sweep on the model (\cref{fig:zhd_artr_oscillation}; the
zero-input free-run is deferred to the darkness comparison below).
\emph{Result:} under constant $\omega$ the
ARTR oscillation period tracks the bump-rotation period $360/|\omega|$
(\textbf{a}) while its amplitude stays roughly constant across the swept range
(\textbf{b}) --- the signature of a carrier slaved to the rotating heading bump
rather than a clock with its own frequency. The model
oscillation is inherited, not intrinsic. This is structural: an intrinsic
rhythm needs L$\leftrightarrow$R inhibition (bistability) and a slow adaptation
variable (to set the $\sim$10--20\,s period), whereas our ARTR is
all-excitatory with $\tau=0.1$\,s and would latch rather than oscillate.

\paragraph{The oscillation is a property of the circuit, not the decoder.}
Repeating the constant-$\omega$ sweep on the $64$-bin-decoder model
(\cref{sec:results_decoder}) gives the same diagnostic
(\cref{fig:zhd_artr_oscillation_kbins}): the ARTR period tracks the
bump-rotation period $360/|\omega|$ and the zero-input free-run decays to
a fixed point. Swapping the $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ readout for a bin
readout therefore leaves the inherited (not intrinsic) character of the
ARTR oscillation unchanged --- consistent with the oscillation being a
property of the recurrent circuit rather than of the heading decoder.

\end{figure}

\paragraph{Darkness: a test for the missing oscillator.}
\emph{Hypothesis:} if the recorded ARTR is intrinsic it keeps oscillating once
the command is removed; the command-driven model does not. \emph{Test:} the
ZAPBench session runs a rotation block straight into a darkness block in the
same fish; drive the model identically (recorded rotation $\omega$, then zero
input) and compare (\cref{fig:zhd_artr_dark}). \emph{Result:} in the dark the
recorded ARTR keeps alternating ($|L-R|$ std $=0.72$) while the model is
near-flat ($0.14$, $\sim$5$\times$ smaller). The cleanest version of the
same test is a zero-input free-run from a small perturbation, with no
preceding drive at all: the model decays to a fixed point (population-mean
amplitude $\approx 0.003$), confirming it carries no self-sustained
oscillation. The recording carries an intrinsic dark-state rhythm
(cf.\ \citealp{dunn2016artr}) the model largely lacks; its much-reduced dark
response is expected by construction, so this is a prediction--data gap that names the
missing ingredients (recurrent inhibition $+$ slow adaptation) --- a
falsifiable target for future work.
