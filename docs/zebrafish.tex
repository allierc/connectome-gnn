\title{\textbf{Zebrafish anterior hindbrain / dIPN: integrating heading
direction, forward velocity, and 2-D position from a single
connectome-locked circuit}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We study a vertebrate self-motion integrator at single-neuron
resolution: the GABAergic \rpi{} population of the larval-zebrafish
anterior hindbrain (\citealp{petrucco2023}), together with the three
afferent populations the \emph{fish2} inventory identifies as carrying
its angular and translational drives. From the \emph{neuprint-fish2}
EM reconstruction we build sign-locked RNN and message-passing GNN
models whose connectivity is fixed to the connectome under an
all-inhibitory Dale convention, and train the same circuit on three
progressively harder integration sub-tasks: heading, heading $+$
forward distance, and 2-D path integration with a dual exteroceptive
$+$ proprioceptive translation afferent. Optimising the
input--output map alone recovers a sharp heading code, but the
preferred-heading map is not anatomically organised and many
connectome-consistent circuits integrate equally well, so the
implementation is degenerate. The task-only model's population
activity is also far from the measured ZAPBench calcium; adding a
calcium-observation loss raises the predicted-vs-recorded per-neuron
correlation from $\rho \approx 0.06$ to $0.4$--$0.55$ while leaving
heading accurately decoded.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In the larval zebrafish, head-direction information is carried by a
population of hundreds of GABAergic inhibitory neurons (the \rpi{} cells)
located in rhombomere~1 of the anterior hindbrain \citep{petrucco2023}.
A sinusoidal ring activity peak travels around the functional ring
(cells ordered by preferred heading, not by anatomical position) in
a direction opposite to the fish's swims, persists for tens of
seconds in the absence of motor activity, and tracks an estimated
heading angle whose magnitude per swim ($\approx \pi/4$~rad) matches
the behaviourally measured turn. The connectivity scheme that
supports the ring activity is the classical ring-attractor
architecture. The electron-microscopy reconstructions of the \rpi{}
axons reveal reciprocal connectivity in the dIPN consistent with
this picture.

\section{Anatomy, functions and connectome}
\label{sec:anatomy}

\paragraph{3-D anatomy.}
\Cref{fig:zhd_3d} shows the circuit cell families rendered in
3-D and colour-coded by the same six-way functional partition used
in the circuit diagram: the
recurrent ring substrate (\emph{dIPN} $=$ \emph{IPNd*} $+$
\emph{IPNds*}); the three afferent populations that the velocity
gate routes the angular and translational drives through
(\emph{ARTR} $=$ \emph{RIPN01/02/03\_a/03\_b}, \emph{pt-IPN1}, and
\emph{motor\_efferent} $=$ \emph{RIPN11/12\_a/12\_c}); and a residual
``other'' category for sub-types not routed through the gate

\emph{dIPN} substrate forms the compact midline neuropil;
\emph{RIPN}-derived afferents descend from the habenula (a paired
diencephalic nucleus relaying limbic and modulatory signals to the
hindbrain) and \emph{pt-IPN}-derived afferents sweep in from the
lateral pretectum (a midbrain visual-processing region carrying optic-
flow and water-flow signals).

\paragraph{Functional circuit.}
\label{method:proprioception}
Forward-swim velocity reaches IPN through two complementary
pathways (\cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{d}): an \emph{exteroceptive}
channel via \emph{pt-IPN1} (optic / lateral-line flow --- delayed
but self-calibrating) and a \emph{proprioceptive} channel via the
motor-efferent RIPN sub-types \emph{RIPN11}, \emph{RIPN12\_a},
\emph{RIPN12\_c} ($37+31$ cells per side, glutamatergic) carrying
the swim command's efference copy (prompt but open-loop). The
proprioception variant adds these cells as a third gate target:
$\omega$ still routes to ARTR, $v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ now drives both
\emph{pt-IPN1} and \emph{motor\_efferent} in parallel, each with its
own pair of learnable scalars. The connectome is unchanged
(\cref{fig:zhd_3d}); only the afferent taxonomy and the gate
architecture differ.

\paragraph{Connectome.}
\Cref{fig:zhd_connectome_summary} summarises depicts the $839$-cell circuits, its binary support $\mathrm{supp}(W^{\mathrm{con}})$
and the signed $W^{\mathrm{con}}$. The matrix is sparse (density $0.032$, $22\,425$
non-zero edges out of $839^2 = 703\,921$). The \rpi{} cells (\emph{IPNd*} and \emph{IPNds*}) are GABAergic
\citep{petrucco2023}; their outgoing weights are sign-flipped and
amplified by 5$\times$. \emph{RIPN*} and \emph{pt-IPN*} have no Dale
annotation on the \emph{fish2} server and are kept positive
(excitatory). Because it is not known whether
the IPN12 cells are GABAergic or glutamatergic, we build two sign
variants from the \emph{same} topology: in \textbf{v1} the IPN12
outgoing weights are Dale-flipped to inhibitory (matching the
\emph{IPNd*}/\emph{IPNds*} ring), and in \textbf{v2} they are left
positive (excitatory).

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\textbf{Recurrent neural network.}\label{method:RNN}
The RNN is a sign-locked discrete-time recurrent network for
$i\in\{1,\dots,N\}$ with $N=731$:
\begin{equation}
\tau\,\frac{d\hat h_i(t)}{dt}
  = -\hat h_i(t)
  + \sum_j \hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij}\,\sigma\!\big(\hat h_j(t)\big)
  + \sum_l \hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{il}\,u_l(t)
  + \hat b_i,
\label{eq:zhd_dynamics}
\end{equation}
$\sigma$ is the sigmoid, $\tau=0.1\,\mathrm{s}$, $\Delta t=0.01\,\mathrm{s}$.
The recurrent matrix is sign-locked to the connectome template
$W^{\mathrm{con}}\in\R^{N\times N}$ (built from the
\emph{neuprint-fish2} reconstruction; outgoing columns of GABAergic
HD-ring cells \emph{IPNd*}, \emph{IPNds*} are negated and amplified
$5\times$): the trainable per-edge parameter is the magnitude
$\hat S$, and
\begin{equation}
\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij}
  = |\hat S_{ij}|\,\mathrm{sign}(W^{\mathrm{con}}_{ij}).
\label{eq:zhd_w_rec}
\end{equation}
External drive enters via $\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}$
(\cref{method:input_extended}) and is read out via
$\hat W^{\mathrm{out}}$ (\cref{method:output_readout}); the ring
cells receive no external input.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Input gate on $\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}$: anatomically licensed afferent ports.}%
\label{method:input_extended}
What enters the network through $\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}$ is fixed by the
afferent partition of \cref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{c}--\textbf{d}: a
channel $c$ may drive only its anatomically licensed subpopulations
$\mathcal{Q}_c$, and the ring cells (\emph{IPNd*}, \emph{IPNds*},
\emph{IPN12}) remain input-free. With $q(i)\in\mathcal{Q}$ the
subpopulation of afferent neuron $i$,
\begin{equation}
\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{i,c} =
\begin{cases}
v_{c,\,q(i)} & i\in\mathcal{A},\ q(i)\in\mathcal{Q}_c,\\
0            & i\in\mathcal{A},\ q(i)\notin\mathcal{Q}_c,\\
\text{free}  & c \in \mathrm{cue},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:zhd_swim_gate}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}$ is the union of afferent neurons. The licensed
ports for the two production-circuit variants are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Q}_\omega=\{\mathrm{ARTR}_{L,R}\},\qquad
\mathcal{Q}_{v_{\mathrm{fwd}}}=
\begin{cases}
\{\mathrm{ptIPN1}_{L,R}\} & (\text{Fig.~\ref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{c}, exteroceptive only}),\\
\{\mathrm{ptIPN1}_{L,R},\,\mathrm{motor\_efferent}_{L,R}\} & (\text{Fig.~\ref{fig:zhd_3d}\textbf{d}, proprioception}).
\end{cases}
\label{eq:zhd_port_map}
\end{equation}
The same rule generalises to the full multi-modal driver across all
nine ZAPBench conditions (\cref{fig:zhd_allblocks}): every visual
channel $c\in\mathrm{vis}$ --- signed rotation/optic-flow velocity
(Rotations, OMR~Turning, Gain), whole-field luminance (Flash), L/R
light difference (Taxis), random-dot motion (Dots) --- takes
$\mathcal{Q}_c=\{\mathrm{ptIPN}_{L,R}\}$, and every self-motion
channel $c\in\mathrm{sm}=(u^{\mathrm{fwd}},u^{\mathrm{turn}})$
derived from the tail EMG takes
$\mathcal{Q}_c=\{\mathrm{RIPN}_{L,R}\}$. Directional channels are
initialised antisymmetrically across hemispheres
($v_{c,L}=-v_{c,R}$) so that leftward and rightward stimuli drive the
ring activity peak in opposite directions; non-directional channels
(forward-swim vigour, whole-field luminance) are initialised
symmetrically. All gates are then free to be retuned --- anatomy
fixes \emph{which port} a modality may use, not the strength or sign
it settles on.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Output readout on $\hat W^{\mathrm{out}}$: task-conditioned columns.}%
\label{method:output_readout}
$\hat W^{\mathrm{out}}$ reads from the HD ring (\emph{IPNd*},
\emph{IPNds*}, \emph{IPN12 pool}) and never from the afferent
populations. Its column dimension $n_{\mathrm{out}}$ is set by the
active sub-task (\cref{fig:zhd_swim_task_modes}):
\begin{equation}
\hat y(t) =
\begin{cases}
(\cos\hat\theta,\sin\hat\theta)                 & n_{\mathrm{out}}=2 \ \ (\text{heading only}),\\
(\cos\hat\theta,\sin\hat\theta,\hat d)          & n_{\mathrm{out}}=3 \ \ (\text{heading}+\text{forward distance}),\\
(\cos\hat\theta,\sin\hat\theta,\hat x,\hat y)   & n_{\mathrm{out}}=4 \ \ (\text{2D path integration}),
\end{cases}
\label{eq:zhd_readout}
\end{equation}
with $\hat y = \hat W^{\mathrm{out}}\,\sigma\!\big(\hat h(t)\big)+\hat b^{\mathrm{out}}$,
$\hat\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\hat y_2,\hat y_1)$. The leaky-vs-perfect choice
(\cref{eq:xi_leaky,eq:xy_pi}) changes only what $\hat d$ and
$(\hat x,\hat y)$ are supervised against, not the readout shape.
The learnable parameters of the model are
\begin{equation*}
\big\{\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij},\;
\{v_{c,q}\}_{c,\,q\in\mathcal{Q}_c},\;
\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{:,\theta_0},\,\hat b,\,
\hat W^{\mathrm{out}},\,\hat b^{\mathrm{out}}\big\};
\end{equation*}
the connectome template $W^{\mathrm{con}}$, the afferent
subpopulation membership, the neuron-type assignments, $\tau$ and
$\Delta t$ are given.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Swim-integration task and target recipes.}%
\label{method:swim_task_modes}\label{method:xi_leaky}
The on-disk swim-integration dataset is a \emph{single superset} from
which several sub-tasks are projected at training time. Swim events are
sampled at a Poisson rate $r_{\mathrm{swim}}=0.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ with
event duration $\Delta t_{\mathrm{swim}}=0.3\,$s, partitioned into four
behavioural categories with fractions $L:R:F:B=0.3:0.3:0.2:0.2$. Left
and right events drive an angular signed phase impulse, forward and
backward events drive a signed forward-velocity impulse. The dataset
writes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a $4$-channel input
    $u\in\R^{B\times T\times 4}=[\,\omega,\,v_{\mathrm{fwd}},\,
    \cos\theta_0\!\cdot\!\delta_{t,0},\,\sin\theta_0\!\cdot\!\delta_{t,0}\,]$
    --- left/right events drive $\omega$, forward/backward events drive
    $v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$, and the initial-heading cue $(\cos\theta_0,\sin\theta_0)$
    is injected as a single-frame impulse at $t=0$ because the
    integrator has no other reference for the absolute heading. There
    is no analogous initial-\emph{position} cue: position is defined
    to start at the origin by construction.
    \item a target tensor whose column layout depends on the sub-task.
    The \emph{scalar} recipe stores
    $y=[\,\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,d\,]$ with circular heading
    $\theta(t)=\theta_0+\int_0^t\omega\,ds$ and a scalar forward-axis
    displacement $d(t)=\int_0^t v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\,ds$ (linear,
    $d(0)=0$). The \emph{2D path-integration} recipe stores
    $y=[\,\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta,\,x,\,y\,]$, where the position is
    obtained by projecting $v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ through the current
    heading and integrating each spatial axis independently
    (\cref{eq:xy_pi}). The 2D recipe is the \emph{true} path-
    integration target: it couples translation to heading, so a
    network has to maintain $\theta$ internally to predict $(x,y)$ at
    all.
\end{itemize}

The default scalar $d$ and the default 2D position $(x, y)$ are both
\emph{perfect} integrators of their respective drives. With a non-zero
mean forward-swim drive these targets grow linearly with the
curriculum horizon, so the loss scales with $T$ and cross-snapshot
comparisons become awkward. A finite time constant $\tau>0$ replaces
the cumulative sum with the leaky recurrence
\begin{equation}
d(t+\Delta t) = \left(1-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_d}\right)d(t)
    + v_{\mathrm{fwd}}(t)\,\Delta t,
\qquad d(0)=0,
\label{eq:xi_leaky}
\end{equation}
equivalent to $\dot d = -d/\tau_d + v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$. For the
2D-position target the same leaky law is applied independently to each
axis,
\begin{equation}
\frac{dx}{dt} = -\frac{x}{\tau_p} + v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\cos\theta,
\qquad
\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{y}{\tau_p} + v_{\mathrm{fwd}}\sin\theta,
\qquad x(0)=y(0)=0,
\label{eq:xy_pi}
\end{equation}
which keeps the trajectory bounded near the recent egocentric
displacement. Biologically this is the natural choice: real neural
integrators leak, and what the fish actually encodes is a
low-pass-filtered estimate of \emph{recent} forward swim vigour and
recent displacement, not absolute long-horizon position. The
production runs use $\tau_d=\tau_p=0.5\,$s, with the
perfect-integrator recipes preserved as separate variants so the loss
and metric comparisons stay clean.

\paragraph{Training.}

\bigskip
Training optimises a per-frame MSE
on the heading readout with two connectome-prior regularisers and
an $\ell_1$ sparsity prior on the recurrent magnitudes,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
= \tfrac{1}{BT}\sum_{b,t} \|\hat y_{b,t} - y_{b,t}\|_2^2
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{cos}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{nf}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}
+ \lambda_{1}\,\|\hat{W}^{rec}_{ij} \|_1,
\label{eq:zhd_loss}
\end{equation}
with block losses computed over type-pair masks
$\mathcal{B}_p \subset \{1,\dots,N\}^2$ (one block per ordered
pre/post neuron-type pair $p \in \mathcal{P}$),
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}
  &= \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}}
     \!\left[1 - \frac{\langle \hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{\mathcal{B}_p},\,
         W^{\mathrm{con}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}\rangle}
       {\|\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}\|\,
        \|W^{\mathrm{con}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}\|}\right],
\label{eq:zhd_cos}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}
  &= \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}}
     \relu\!\big(\kappa -
       \overline{|\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}|}\big)^2.
\label{eq:zhd_nf}
\end{align}
The cosine-distance term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}$ anchors each
block to the connectome template; the norm-floor term
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}$ prevents any type-pair block from
collapsing to zero by penalising the squared shortfall of the block
mean absolute weight $\overline{|\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{\mathcal{B}_p}|}$
below a fixed floor $\kappa$ (typical value $\kappa = 0.05$). Because
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}$ is scale-invariant,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}$ is required to prevent a degenerate
solution in which the cosine prior is satisfied by an arbitrarily
small recurrent magnitude. The type-pair vocabulary $\mathcal{P}$ is
the cartesian product of the 33 fish2 cell types identified in
\cref{sec:anatomy}, restricted to pairs with at least one non-zero
entry in $W^{\mathrm{con}}$.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Calcium observation loss.}\label{method:obs}
The heading loss of \cref{eq:zhd_loss} constrains only the
two-dimensional readout $\hat y(t)$; the $N$-dimensional recurrent state
$\hat h(t)$ is left unobserved, so the task alone does not pin the
internal dynamics. To constrain the implementation we add an optional
term that matches the model's predicted calcium to \emph{recorded}
activity. A GCaMP indicator turns the model's per-neuron activity into a
fluorescence trace by causal convolution with a unit-area
difference-of-exponentials kernel,
\begin{equation}
k_{\mathrm{GCaMP}}(t) \;\propto\; e^{-t/\tau_d} - e^{-t/\tau_r},
  \quad t \ge 0,
\qquad
\hat c_i(t) \;=\; \big(k_{\mathrm{GCaMP}} * \hat h_i\big)(t),
\label{eq:zhd_gcamp}
\end{equation}
with nominal cytosolic GCaMP7f rise/decay constants
$(\tau_r,\tau_d) = (0.15,\,1.20)\,\mathrm{s}$ \citep{dana2019}. ZAPBench
imaged the slower nuclear \emph{H2B-GCaMP7f}, so \cref{eq:zhd_gcamp} if
anything \emph{under}-states the low-pass smearing of the real observable.
The kernel is normalised to unit area so the transform
preserves the mean, the convolution is evaluated on the model grid
$\Delta t$, and it is differentiable, so gradients flow from the
fluorescence back into $\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}$, the afferent gate and
$\hat b$. Let $\mathcal{O}$ index the $|\mathcal{O}| = 481$ HD-circuit
neurons matched to a recorded ROI (below) and let $c^{\star}_i(t)$ be the
recorded $\Delta F/F$ of neuron $i$. Because the indicator gain and
baseline are not identifiable, the term is made invariant to a per-neuron
affine rescaling by $z$-scoring both signals over time before comparison,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}}
  = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{O}|\,B_c T}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}}\sum_{b,t}
    \big(z[\hat c_i]_{b,t} - z[c^{\star}_i]_{b,t}\big)^2 ,
\qquad
z[x]_{b,t} = \frac{x_{b,t} - \mu_{b,i}}{\sigma_{b,i}},
\label{eq:zhd_obs}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{b,i},\sigma_{b,i}$ are the per-trial, per-neuron mean and
standard deviation over time. For unit-variance signals
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}} = 2\,(1-\rho)$ with $\rho$ the Pearson
correlation, so the term rewards temporal \emph{shape} agreement rather
than absolute scale. The full objective adds it to \cref{eq:zhd_loss}
with weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}$,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathcal{L}
  + \lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}}$;
setting $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$ (or supplying no calcium trials)
recovers the task-only model exactly.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{ZAPBench training data.}\label{method:zapbench}
The recorded activity is the publicly released ZAPBench whole-brain
light-sheet calcium recording of a larval zebrafish, registered to the
same \emph{fish2} EM volume as the connectome (\cref{sec:anatomy}): the
$\Delta F/F$ trace of every segmented cell is taken from the ZAPBench
release and each connectome \emph{bodyId} is matched to its functional
ROI through the EM\,$\leftrightarrow$\,functional registration. Of the
$839$ neurons in the IPN12 circuit, $481$ carry a matched recording ---
$300$ ring-pool cells (\emph{IPNd}/\emph{IPNds}/\emph{IPN12}) and $181$
afferents (\emph{RIPN}, \emph{pt-IPN}). The observation loss is taken over
all $481$ matched cells; the real-vs-model kinographs display only the
$300$ ring-pool subset. We supervise on the ``Rotations'' block, in which a
$45^\circ/\mathrm{s}$ drifting grating rotates the animal's virtual
heading: the true heading is read from the recorded stimulus channel and
differentiated to the angular-velocity drive $s(t)$, so the \emph{model}
is driven by the \emph{same} stimulus that produced the recording. The
block spans ${\sim}600\,\mathrm{s}$ ($656$ imaging frames at the
$1.09\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $0.915\,\mathrm{s}$ imaging rate). Because the model
runs at $\Delta t = 0.01\,\mathrm{s}$, the recorded $\Delta F/F$ is
linearly interpolated up to the model grid (edge-padded), and each neuron
is $z$-scored \emph{globally} over the whole block --- not per window ---
so the target keeps a single, trial-independent scale. The block is then
sliced into $1000$-frame ($10\,\mathrm{s}$) trials drawn from disjoint
train/test time spans, in the same format as the synthetic
swim-integration trials: channel~0 is the recorded drive $s(t)$ and the
first frame carries the $(\cos\theta_0,\sin\theta_0)$ heading cue, with no
warm-up. During training each task minibatch is augmented with $B_c$ of
these calcium trials in a single forward pass; the heading MSE of
\cref{eq:zhd_loss} is taken over the combined batch (the rotation trials
also carry a true heading), while $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is
evaluated on the calcium trials only.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Graph neural network model.}\label{method:GNN}
The GNN model replaces the dense recurrent operator
$\sum_j \hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{ij}\,\sigma(\hat h_j)$ of
\cref{eq:zhd_dynamics} with a message-passing graph neural network
(Gilmer \emph{et al.}\ 2017), and --- crucially --- does \emph{not}
hard-code either the leak, the firing-rate nonlinearity, or the
synaptic activation function: the linear leak $-\hat h_i/\tau$, the
sigmoid $\sigma$, and the per-edge message that combines a
presynaptic activity with its postsynaptic effect must all be
discovered from the input--output map inside the learned drift
$f_\theta$ and the learned signalling function $g_\phi$. Beyond
removing this functional bias, the GNN also admits an explicit form
of neuronal heterogeneity through a per-neuron learnable embedding
$\mathbf a_i \in \R^d$ that enters both $f_\theta$ and $g_\phi$:
same-type neurons share their architectural role but can specialise
via their embedding, so a single pair of MLPs covers the $31$ fish2
cell types of \cref{sec:anatomy} and all $N = 731$ instance-level
distinctions without any further per-type parameter tying; we use
$d = 4$ as the default. Nodes correspond to neurons and edges to
the non-zero entries of the zebrafish HD connectome template
$W^{\mathrm{con}}$, and the subthreshold activity evolves as
\begin{equation}
\tau\,\frac{d\hat h_i(t)}{dt}
  = f_\theta\!\Big(\hat h_i(t),\,\mathbf a_i,\,
       \sum_{j \in \mathcal N_i}
       \hat W_{ij}\,g_\phi\!\big(\hat h_j(t),\mathbf a_j\big)\Big)
  + \sum_l \hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{il}\,u_l(t),
\label{eq:zhd_gnn_dynamics}
\end{equation}
with the same readout (\cref{eq:zhd_readout}), swim-gated encoder
(\cref{eq:zhd_swim_gate}), and dynamics constants ($\tau,\Delta t$)
as the RNN. The neighbour set is fixed by the connectome topology,
$\mathcal N_i = \{j : W^{\mathrm{con}}_{ij} \neq 0\}$. The node
update $f_\theta$ and edge message $g_\phi$ are three-layer
perceptrons (width 64, ReLU, linear output). $g_\phi$ maps the
presynaptic state $\hat h_j$ to a per-edge message conditioned on
$\mathbf a_j$ and weighted by the per-edge gain $\hat W_{ij}$;
$f_\theta$ then processes the postsynaptic state $\hat h_i$, the
embedding $\mathbf a_i$, and the aggregated synaptic drive. We
constrain $g_\phi$ to be monotonically increasing in $\hat h_j$ via
the prior $\partial g_\phi / \partial \hat h \ge 0$ (see
\cref{eq:zhd_gnn_loss}); combined with the sign-locked $\hat W_{ij}$
(\cref{eq:zhd_gnn_w}) this restores Dale's law at the per-edge level
without introducing a $\pm$-sign degeneracy. The leak, the
firing-rate non-linearity, and any departure from a linear synapse
must emerge from $(f_\theta, g_\phi, \mathbf a)$ themselves; the
monotonicity priors below provide the minimum inductive bias for
$f_\theta, g_\phi$ to behave like a neuron (postsynaptic drive
increases with net excitatory input). The per-edge weight is
sign-locked to the connectome as in the RNN,
\begin{equation}
\hat W_{ij}
  = |W_{ij}|\,\mathrm{sign}(W^{\mathrm{con}}_{ij}),
\label{eq:zhd_gnn_w}
\end{equation}
where $W_{ij} \in \R$ is the trainable per-edge magnitude.
In the zebrafish circuit $\mathrm{sign}(W^{\mathrm{con}}_{ij})$ is
negative for every edge whose presynaptic neuron is in the dIPN
(\emph{IPNd*} or \emph{IPNds*}) and positive for every edge from
\emph{RIPN*} or \emph{pt-IPN*}; the GNN therefore inherits the
inhibitory dIPN directly from the sign pattern. The learnable parameters are
\begin{equation*}
\big\{\theta,\,\phi,\,\mathbf a,\,\hat{W}_{ij},\;
v_{\mathrm{RIPN}_L},\,v_{\mathrm{RIPN}_R},\,
v_{\mathrm{ptIPN}_L},\,v_{\mathrm{ptIPN}_R},\;
\hat W^{\mathrm{in}}_{:,\theta_0},\,\hat b,\,
\hat W^{\mathrm{out}},\,\hat b^{\mathrm{out}}\big\};
\end{equation*}
the connectome template $W^{\mathrm{con}}$, the
RIPN / pt-IPN subpopulation membership, the neuron-type assignments,
$\tau$, $\Delta t$, and the mediolateral ring ordering used by the
decoder are given.

As with the RNN, heading recovery from cos/sin readouts is
fundamentally non-unique: many distinct $(\theta,\phi,\mathbf a,W)$
configurations produce identical readouts. We constrain the solution
space with the same connectome-prior regularisers as the RNN
($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}}$ from
\cref{eq:zhd_cos,eq:zhd_nf}, computed on the per-edge gain surface
$\hat W_{ij}$ with floor threshold $\kappa$), together with sparsity
and monotonicity priors on the MLPs:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L} \;=\;&
  \tfrac{1}{BT}\sum_{b,t}\|\hat y_{b,t} - y_{b,t}\|_2^2
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{cos}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cos}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{nf}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{nf}} \nonumber\\
& +\;\lambda_0\|\theta\|_1 + \lambda_1\|\phi\|_1
   + \lambda_2\|\theta\| + \lambda_3\|\phi\|
   \;+\;\gamma_0\|W\|_1 + \gamma_1\|W\|_2 \nonumber\\
& +\;\mu_0\!\left\|
       \relu\!\left(+\tfrac{\partial f_\theta(\hat h,\mathbf a,m)}
                          {\partial \hat h}\right)\right\|
   +\;\mu_1\!\left\|
       \relu\!\left(-\tfrac{\partial f_\theta(\hat h,\mathbf a,m)}
                          {\partial m}\right)\right\| \nonumber\\
& +\;\mu_2\!\left\|
       \relu\!\left(-\tfrac{\partial g_\phi(\hat h,\mathbf a)}
                          {\partial \hat h}\right)\right\|
   +\;\mu_3\,\big\|g_\phi(h_\star,\mathbf a)-h_\star\big\|,
\label{eq:zhd_gnn_loss}
\end{align}
where $m = \sum_{j \in \mathcal N_i}\hat W_{ij}\,
g_\phi(\hat h_j,\mathbf a_j)$ is the aggregated synaptic input. The
$\ell_1$ penalties $\lambda_0,\lambda_1$ on the MLP weights bias
$f_\theta, g_\phi$ toward sparser, simpler functions; the $\ell_2$
penalties $\lambda_2,\lambda_3$ prevent weight explosion. The
leak-monotonicity term $\mu_0$ penalises \emph{positive} slopes of
$f_\theta$ in the postsynaptic state $\hat h$, encoding the prior
that the drift contains a leak
($\partial f_\theta / \partial \hat h \le 0$); $\mu_1$ penalises
negative slopes of $f_\theta$ in the aggregated input $m$, encoding
the prior that postsynaptic drive increases with net excitatory
input. Together, $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ force the discovered drift to be
decreasing in self-voltage and increasing in synaptic input --- the
two qualitative signatures of \cref{eq:zhd_dynamics}. The term $\mu_2$
enforces the analogous monotonicity prior on $g_\phi$ in the
presynaptic voltage. The normalisation term $\mu_3$ anchors $g_\phi$
at a reference subthreshold value $h_\star$, removing the
multiplicative degeneracy between $g_\phi$ and $\hat W$ in
\cref{eq:zhd_gnn_dynamics}. Hyperparameters
$\lambda_{\{0,1,2,3\}},\,\gamma_{\{0,1\}},\,\mu_{\{0,1,2,3\}}$ and the
MLP widths/depths are tuned with an agentic optimisation procedure,
with $r_{\mathrm{roll},1k}$ --- the Pearson correlation between the decoded
and true heading over a $1\,000$-step deterministic swim-impulse rollout
--- as the primary score.

\paragraph{Mutual-information estimators.}\label{method:MI}
We use mutual information to quantify how strongly each unit's
firing rate $r_i(t) = \sigma(\hat h_i(t))$ tracks the heading
$\theta(t)$ (or, where stated, the angular velocity $\omega(t)$ or
the swim-onset trace $s(t)$) over a held-out rollout. For a single
neuron we wrap $\theta$ to $(-\pi, \pi]$ via
$\theta \leftarrow \arg(e^{i\theta})$, discretise the wrapped angle
into $n_\theta = 32$ equal-width angular bins and $r$ into $n_r = 20$
equal-width bins, then compute the plug-in estimator in bits,
\begin{equation}
\hat I(r_i;\theta) = \sum_{b,c} \hat p(b,c)\,\log_2
\frac{\hat p(b,c)}{\hat p(b)\,\hat p(c)},
\label{eq:zhd_mi_plugin}
\end{equation}
where $\hat p$ is the empirical joint over the
$(\theta\!\!-\!\!\mathrm{bin}, r\!\!-\!\!\mathrm{bin})$ histogram. The
plug-in construction is used so that the per-neuron and
per-cell-type quantities of
\cref{sec:results_four_classes} are
comparable across cell types and sub-populations. For a group of $K$ neurons we
use the cross-validated lower bound
\begin{equation}
\hat I_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mathbf r_{1:K};\theta) =
H(\theta) - \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal D_{\mathrm{te}}|}
\sum_{(t,\theta)\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{te}}}
-\log_2 q_\psi(\theta_b\mid \mathbf r(t)),
\label{eq:zhd_mi_joint_lb}
\end{equation}
where $q_\psi$ is an $L_2$-regularised multinomial logistic
regression with input $\mathbf r \in \mathbb R^K$ and output the
$n_\theta$ heading bins, trained on a stratified 5-fold split.

\paragraph{Image-domain ROI sampling and $\Delta F/F_0$.}\label{method:dff}
To make the model's population dynamics comparable to two-photon
calcium imaging of the larval-zebrafish dIPN, we sample activity
\emph{from the rendered frames} of the dorsal-view animation, not
from the latent state $\hat h_i(t)$. Each frame is a
$3000 \times 4380$-pixel canvas in which each neuron's skeleton is
drawn with a per-segment alpha proportional to its firing rate; we
place four axis-aligned ROI rectangles $R_k$
($k \in \{\mathrm{dIPN}_L, \mathrm{dIPN}_R, \mathrm{dsIPN}_L,
\mathrm{dsIPN}_R\}$) over the dorsal-IPN neuropil (drawn on frame~0
of \emph{\figdir/3D\_voltage\_const/frame\_0000.png}) and
integrate the green channel of the canvas RGBA buffer:
\begin{equation}
I_k(t) \;=\; \frac{1}{|R_k|}\sum_{(x,y)\,\in\,R_k}
              \mathrm{img}_t[y, x, \mathrm{green}].
\label{eq:zhd_roi_intens}
\end{equation}
The four traces $I_k(t)$ are then converted to a fractional change
with respect to a post-init baseline, mirroring the Seelig / Kim /
Green calcium-imaging convention:
\begin{align}
F_0[k] &\;=\; \bigl\langle I_k(t)\bigr\rangle_{t \,\ge\,
                t_{\mathrm{init}}} + \varepsilon, \\
\Delta F/F_0\,(k,t) &\;=\;
   \frac{I_k(t) - F_0[k]}{F_0[k]},
\label{eq:zhd_dff}
\end{align}
with $t_{\mathrm{init}} = 0.5$~s (drops the network start-up
transient that would otherwise dominate the baseline) and
$\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$. The per-pair population-vector position
overlaid on the kinographs in red is the activity-weighted centre
of mass across each $L/R$ pair,
$\mathrm{pos}_p(t) = \big(I_{p,L}\,y_{p,L} + I_{p,R}\,y_{p,R}\big)/
                      \big(I_{p,L} + I_{p,R}\big)$,
i.e.\ the same Seelig/Hulse PVA reduced to two phase bins per pair.
Image-domain sampling inherits all the projection biases of the
microscope (depth-of-field, projection, downsample) and so the
kinographs are directly comparable to \petrucco{}~Fig.~2 without
having to invert the calcium kernel.

readout therefore fits the swim-event training distribution well
but fails to extrapolate to a sustained constant drive --- heading
is not supervised in this variant, so the recurrent dynamics lose
the absolute-distance anchor, and the integration-gain panel is
left blank.

\paragraph{Summary across variants.}
All seven runs converge to a working integrator on the same connectome.
The leaky variants do not improve the per-frame R² compared with
their perfect counterparts on the joint tasks (both reach $\geq 0.999$),
but they bound the supervisory target across the curriculum horizon
and remove the linear-loss-scaling artefact of the perfect integrator,
which makes cross-snapshot comparisons cleaner. The compass behaviour
is robust to the task complexity: adding forward-distance or 2-D
position regression alongside heading does not degrade the
constant-$\omega$ integration gain (panel i is unit-slope in every
heading-decoding run).

\subsection{Angular / displacement partition of the recurrent pool}
\label{sec:results_four_classes}

\paragraph{Angular / displacement partition (recurrent pool only).}
The joint-target variants
(\cref{fig:zhd_evolution_both,fig:zhd_evolution_both_leaky,fig:zhd_evolution_position2d,fig:zhd_evolution_position2d_leaky})
let us ask which \emph{recurrent} neurons carry information about
\emph{which} target. The analysis is restricted to the recurrent pool
(\emph{IPNd*}, \emph{IPNds*}, \emph{IPN12}; $n=551$ of the $839$
cells); the afferents (ARTR, pt-IPN1, motor\_efferent) are excluded
by construction, since their activity is driven directly by the
gate-injected $\omega$ or $v_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ and their MI on the
corresponding integrated quantity is trivially high. For each
recurrent neuron $i$ we compute the per-neuron mutual information on
the heading $I(\hat h_i;\theta)$ and on the forward distance
$I(\hat h_i;d)$ from a concatenation of $64$ held-out test trials of
the position-2D leaky model (10\,s each, $1{,}000$ frames; circular
MI for $\theta$, plug-in MI for $d$). Splitting each axis at its
recurrent-pool median assigns every cell to one of four quadrants:
\emph{angular} ($I(\hat h_i;\theta) \ge \tau_\theta$, $I(\hat h_i;d) < \tau_d$),
\emph{displacement} ($I(\hat h_i;\theta) < \tau_\theta$, $I(\hat h_i;d) \ge \tau_d$),
\emph{shared} (high on both), and a residual \emph{neither} group.
On the position-2D leaky checkpoint this gives
$\text{angular}/\text{displacement}/\text{shared}/\text{neither}
= 37/37/239/238$ --- the recurrent pool splits into a dominant
shared population and a small target-specialised tail. This new
partition replaces the earlier R/L/D/Z labelling: it is
target-symmetric (rotation and translation are on the same footing),
restricted to the cells that actually \emph{integrate}, and uses
only model activity (no readout weights).

\paragraph{Edge-block analysis: micro-circuits or distributed?}
On the same checkpoint we compute the mean magnitude
$\langle|\hat W^{\mathrm{rec}}_{p\to q}|\rangle$ over recurrent$\to$
recurrent connectome edges between the three pools $p, q \in
\{\text{angular}, \text{shared}, \text{displacement}\}$ (the
\emph{neither} pool is dropped from the edge analysis). The
resulting $3 \times 3$ matrix
(\cref{fig:zhd_mi_partition}\textbf{b}) is \emph{not} block-diagonal:
the off-diagonal angular$\leftrightarrow$displacement entries
($0.466$ and $0.644$) are comparable to the within-pool diagonals
($0.622$ angular$\to$angular, $0.734$ displacement$\to$displacement),
and the \emph{shared} pool exchanges substantial weight with both
endpoints. The integrated heading and integrated distance are
therefore \emph{not} computed on disjoint sub-circuits; the two
codes are carried by overlapping recurrent neurons connected by
edges of similar magnitude across the partition. The dominant
within-pool block is shared$\to$displacement ($0.766$), consistent
with the displacement signal being read out of the same recurrent
substrate that also encodes heading.

\subsection{Single-cell-type ablations}
\label{sec:results_ablation}

To ask whether any single cell type is individually required for the
heading computation, we lesioned each of the 33 \emph{fish2} subtypes in
turn --- masking all of its incoming and outgoing edges out of the
connectome and \emph{retraining the circuit from scratch} with the lesion
held fixed --- and compared the converged swim-integration rollout to the
unablated \textbf{v1} control (\cref{fig:zhd_ablation}). The compass is
robust to any single-type lesion: every knockout still solves the task, with
held-out path-integration accuracy $\ge 0.99$ in all 33 runs and integration
gain within $3\%$ of unity. To bound the natural spread of these metrics we
retrained the unablated circuit ten times from fresh seeds (shared data);
over these ten folds $\mathrm{pi\_acc}=0.994\pm0.004$ and
$|\mathrm{gain}-1|=0.032\pm0.013$ (mean\,$\pm$\,SD), and the precision score
--- the per-trial heading RMSE over the held-out test trials ($512$ per run)
--- is $5.6\pm1.4^\circ$ (mean\,$\pm$\,SD over the ten folds). A lesion is
therefore significant only if it leaves the $\pm 2.8^\circ$ ($2$\,SD) seed
band (grey in \cref{fig:zhd_ablation}a), and none of the 33 knockouts does:
the most affected (\emph{IPN12\_b}, $7.0^\circ$) and the least
(\emph{RIPN12\_c}, $3.0^\circ$) both sit inside the band, so the per-type
spread is fully contained within the seed-to-seed spread, and every
\emph{apparent improvement} is likewise a within-band fluctuation rather than
a real benefit. The integration gain tells the same story
(\cref{fig:zhd_ablation}b): every knockout keeps $\langle|g-1|\rangle$ inside
its own fold band, so heading integration stays linear and near unit-gain
whichever type is removed --- no single subtype is a structural bottleneck,
and the connectome carries enough redundancy that the optimiser re-routes the
integration around the lesion. Although nothing clears the band, the ranking
is orderly: the dorsal-IPN recurrent cells dominate the high-RMSE end (both
\emph{IPN12\_a}/\emph{IPN12\_b} and the \emph{IPNd13} cluster), while the
afferents --- the habenular \emph{RIPN*} and pretectal \emph{pt-IPN*} --- sit
at the low-RMSE end, several below the unablated baseline. This is the
expected polarity: the recurrent cells carry the linear readout
(\cref{sec:results_four_classes}), so losing one nudges precision the most,
whereas an afferent input can be re-routed during retraining --- consistent
with the pretectum, the dominant velocity carrier in the \emph{trained}
model, being structurally dispensable. The effect is a tendency, not a
result; lifting any individual type above the seed band would need a per-seed
ablation ensemble.

\subsection{Task ability does not imply realistic activity}
\label{sec:results_obs_baseline}

The results so far establish that the connectome-constrained network learns
to integrate heading accurately, and that the solution is robust to any
single-type lesion. Accuracy on the task, however, says nothing about
whether the network's \emph{internal activity} matches the brain. We test
this directly by driving the trained baseline model
(\emph{zebrafish\_hd\_si\_ipn12\_v1}, trained on the swim-integration task
\emph{only}) with the recorded ZAPBench ``Rotations'' stimulus
(\cref{method:zapbench}), converting its rolled-out activity to GCaMP7f
calcium (\cref{eq:zhd_gcamp}), and comparing it to the recorded
$\Delta F/F$ of the $300$ matched ring-pool neurons in the same
rastermap row order (\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}).

The model is an excellent head-direction predictor --- its decoded heading
tracks the $45^\circ/\mathrm{s}$ rotation closely
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}b, bottom) --- yet its population activity is
plainly \emph{not} the recorded one: the learned kinograph is a clean,
stimulus-locked set of bands, whereas the recording
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}a) is irregular, bursty and drift-laden. This
is the degeneracy at the heart of the paper, now read out on real data:
\emph{many circuits realise the same head-direction computation, and hence
the same task ability, while producing different activity}. Task
supervision constrains only the two-dimensional readout
(\cref{eq:zhd_readout}); the $N$-dimensional state it leaves unobserved is
exactly where implementations diverge.

A natural objection is that the mismatch is merely cosmetic: perhaps the
recorded activity still encodes heading, just in a form our eye does not
read off the kinograph. We tested this directly by training a decoder ---
a small neural network --- to predict the instantaneous heading
$(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ from the recorded per-frame $\Delta F/F$ of the
$300$ ring-pool neurons (fit on the first $70\%$ of the rotation block,
evaluated on the held-out tail). The result was \emph{not} conclusive: the
decoded heading (black in \cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}a) does not track the
true rotation (green), in sharp contrast to the model's own readout, which
recovers heading almost perfectly from its latent state
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}b). The implication is that \emph{the
instantaneous head-direction signal is hard to read, frame by frame, from
the observed calcium activity}.

How much of this failure is the \emph{observable} rather than the brain? We
settle the confound with a control: run the \emph{identical} decoder on the
trained model pushed through the \emph{same} observation pipeline --- its
rolled-out activity convolved with the GCaMP kernel, sampled at the
$1.09\,\mathrm{Hz}$ imaging rate and restricted to the same $300$ cells
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_control}). The model provably encodes heading: its latent
readout decodes the rotation at circular $r = 0.97$. Through the degraded
observable the \emph{same} circuit decodes at only $r = 0.73$ --- the
observation pipeline alone (GCaMP low-pass of the fast $45^\circ/\mathrm{s}$
ring activity, $\tau_d = 1.2\,\mathrm{s}$, \cref{eq:zhd_gcamp}; $1.09\,\mathrm{Hz}$
aliasing of an $8\,\mathrm{s}$ revolution; only $300$ of $839$ cells seen)
removes a substantial fraction of the per-frame legibility. It does
\emph{not}, however, reach the real-data level ($r = 0.14$): a circuit that
encoded heading as cleanly as the model would still be decodable at
$r \approx 0.73$ under the recording's observation, so the observable is a
major but \emph{not} the sole cause. The residual gap reflects additional
real-world degradation --- recording noise, behavioural variability of the
animal relative to the grating, and the idealised, stimulus-locked model
ring activity being crisper than the brain's. Either way heading is carried in the
population's \emph{temporal dynamics}, not in any single calcium frame ---
which is why the observation loss matches the time-course of each neuron's
trace (\cref{eq:zhd_obs}, a per-neuron shape loss) rather than asking the
activity to decode heading frame-by-frame.

This motivates the calcium observation loss (\cref{eq:zhd_obs}): by adding
a term that pulls the model's predicted calcium toward the recorded
$\Delta F/F$, we aim to \emph{partially break} this degeneracy ---
selecting, among the task-equivalent circuits, those whose activity is
also consistent with the measured brain. Runs that train with the
observation loss are reported in \cref{sec:results_obs_sweep};
\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline} is the no-observation-loss
($\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$) baseline against which they are compared.

\subsection{Calcium-observation loss partially closes the activity gap}
\label{sec:results_obs_sweep}

We now turn the observation loss on. Using the GNN model
(\cref{method:GNN}) we trained the $839$-cell circuit on the
swim-integration task (dataset~A) while augmenting each minibatch with
$B_c$ recorded ``Rotations'' trials (dataset~B) and matching predicted
to recorded calcium with the $z$-scored shape loss of \cref{eq:zhd_obs},
sweeping the observation weight
$\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} \in \{0,\,0.1,\,0.5,\,1,\,5\}$
($\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}=0$ is the task-only control). Each trained model is then driven by the
held-out recorded stimulus and its rolled-out activity rendered to
GCaMP7f calcium exactly as for the baseline (\cref{fig:zhd_obs_baseline}),
and scored against the recorded $\Delta F/F$ over a continuous
$600\,\mathrm{s}$ rollout (\cref{tab:zhd_obs_sweep},
\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction}). The figure of merit is the per-neuron
temporal $z$-MSE of \cref{eq:zhd_obs}, equal to $2(1-\rho)$ with $\rho$
the predicted-vs-recorded Pearson correlation, reported separately for
the $300$ ring-pool cells and the $181$ afferents.

The loss does what it is designed to. With $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$
the task-only model's calcium is essentially uncorrelated with the
recording ($z$-MSE $\approx 1.88$ for both pools, $\rho \approx 0.06$):
the network integrates heading perfectly yet its activity is unrelated to
the brain's, exactly the degeneracy of \cref{sec:results_obs_baseline}.
Turning the loss on collapses this error immediately --- already at the
smallest weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0.1$ the ring-pool $z$-MSE
drops to $1.25$ ($\rho \approx 0.38$) and the afferent pool to $0.92$
($\rho \approx 0.54$) --- and larger weights buy little further reduction
($z$-MSE plateaus at $1.1$--$1.3$ for the ring activity peak and $0.90$--$0.98$ for the
afferents across $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} \in [0.1, 5]$;
\cref{fig:zhd_obs_compare} contrasts the off and strong extremes
directly). Most of the
benefit is therefore captured by the mere presence of the term rather
than its magnitude. The afferent (\emph{RIPN}/\emph{pt-IPN}) pool is
reconstructed markedly better than the recurrent pool (afferent
$\rho \approx 0.54$ and SSIM up to $0.44$, vs.\ ring-pool $\rho \approx 0.40$
and SSIM $\approx 0.18$), consistent with the afferents carrying the
stimulus-locked part of the recording while the ring activity peak is the irregular,
drift-laden activity that is hardest to match.

The fit is partial, not complete, and trades against the task at large
weights. Even at its best the ring-pool correlation plateaus near
$\rho \approx 0.4$: the loss pulls the predicted activity toward the
recording but does not reproduce it, so the degeneracy of
\cref{sec:results_obs_baseline} is \emph{partially}, not fully, broken
--- the expected outcome for a single per-neuron shape loss applied to
$481$ of the $839$ cells. Pushing $\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}$ higher does not
help and begins to hurt: the structural SSIM peaks at the smallest weight
($\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0.1$) and declines thereafter, and the
decoded-heading panels (\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction}, bottom)
develop more frequent slips as the observation term competes with the
heading MSE. An intermediate weight
($\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} \approx 0.1$--$0.5$) is thus the operating point
that best balances calcium realism against task accuracy.

\subsection{Signals not yet accounted for: the fish's own behaviour}
\label{sec:results_unmodeled}

The observation loss closes only part of the gap --- the ring-pool
correlation plateaus near $\rho \approx 0.4$
(\cref{tab:zhd_obs_sweep}) and the continuous rollout stays visibly more
periodic and more deterministic than the recording
(\cref{fig:zhd_obs_reconstruction}). This ceiling is not only a property
of the loss; it also reflects what the model is \emph{allowed to see}.
The only time-varying input to the network is the angular-velocity drive
$s(t)$, which for the Rotations block is an essentially periodic
$\pm 45^\circ/\mathrm{s}$ square wave. A deterministic circuit driven by a
periodic input can only produce (after transients) a periodic output, so
\emph{any} non-periodic structure in the recorded $\Delta F/F$ is, by
construction, outside the model's reach --- and the recorded ring activity
activity is conspicuously non-periodic across the $600\,\mathrm{s}$ block
even though the stimulus repeats.

\Cref{fig:zhd_unmodeled} lays the recording's covariates side by side over
the rotation block to make the missing signals explicit. Above the
recorded $\Delta F/F$ we plot, from the raw ZAPBench ephys, the fish's own
swimming behaviour --- the quantity the model never receives. The two
bottom traces are the left and right tail-muscle EMG channels (the
independent motor readout); the larval-zebrafish swim decode summarises
them as a \emph{forward} drive (bilateral envelope) and a \emph{turning}
signal (left/right asymmetry). These behave very differently with respect
to the stimulus. Turning is largely stimulus-locked --- it correlates with
the drive at $\rho \approx 0.76$, the expected optomotor following of the
grating --- and so a stimulus-driven model can in principle capture it
(consistent with the better-reconstructed, stimulus-locked afferent pool,
\cref{sec:results_obs_sweep}). Forward drive, by contrast, is a slow,
non-periodic latent: its lag-one autocorrelation is $\approx 0.97$ and it
is essentially uncorrelated with the stimulus magnitude
($|\rho| \lesssim 0.05$). It drifts on the tens-of-seconds-to-minutes
timescale that dominates the recurrent pool's irregular activity, yet nothing
in $s(t)$ carries it.\footnote{The forward/turning traces are derived from
the two tail-EMG electrodes by the standard ZAPBench swim-EMG pipeline
(windowed-variance envelope, swim-bout detection, per-bout weighting);
we verified they are genuinely those channels by recovering
\emph{forward} from the bilateral EMG sum ($\rho \approx 0.57$) and
\emph{turning} from the left$-$right difference ($\rho \approx 0.42$). The
per-frame RMS shown here is a coarse envelope of the same channels.}

Three classes of signal are therefore present in the recording but absent
from the current model. (i) \emph{Self-generated motor drive} --- the
forward/turning behaviour above, which is internally generated and not a
deterministic function of the visual stimulus; the forward component in
particular injects exactly the slow, aperiodic variation the model cannot
synthesise. (ii) \emph{Slow physiological and state drift} --- bleaching,
arousal, and adaptation accumulate over the block, which is why even the
per-trial--stitched reconstruction (re-anchored every $10\,\mathrm{s}$)
matches better than the continuous $600\,\mathrm{s}$ rollout, and why the
per-trial $z$-scoring in \cref{eq:zhd_obs} silently removes this component
rather than fitting it. (iii) \emph{Unmodelled sensory channels} --- the
recording also carries the raw visual-velocity and other stimulus channels
at full temporal resolution, of which the model receives only the distilled
heading drive. None of these are failures of identifiability: they are
inputs the network is simply not given. The natural next step --- and the
motivation for the per-trial timestamp now stored alongside dataset~B ---
is to provide the model with these covariates (the behavioural drive
and/or an explicit slow-time input) so that the aperiodic, behaviour-locked
structure becomes something it can express rather than something the loss
must average away. We leave this conditioning to future work.

\section{Outlook: one circuit, many tasks across the ZAPBench conditions}
\label{sec:outlook}

\paragraph{The idea, in plain terms.}
The Rotations block is one of \emph{nine} conditions in the same continuous
recording of the same $481$ neurons (\cref{fig:zhd_allblocks}); we have so far
used only the $600\,\mathrm{s}$ rotation slice. The contradiction of
\cref{sec:results_obs_baseline} --- the model decodes heading from its readout
at $\rho = 0.97$, yet heading cannot be read frame-by-frame from the recorded
activity ($\rho = 0.14$, \cref{fig:zhd_obs_control}) --- only arises if one
assumes heading must live in the \emph{raw} per-neuron activity. It need not.
If each cell carries a \emph{mixture} of two variables --- heading
$\theta(t)$ and a second, behaviourally meaningful variable $d(t)$ that is
roughly orthogonal to heading in population space --- then a \emph{tuned}
readout that projects onto the heading subspace recovers $\theta$ cleanly
(as the model's own readout does), while a \emph{naive} per-frame decoder,
seeing $d$ as nuisance variance, cannot. Two signals share one population
the way two radio stations share one wire: with the right filter each is
legible, without it the sum is garbled. Crucially, if $d$ is itself slow and
aperiodic, mixing it in makes the population activity aperiodic --- exactly the
recorded signature the heading-only model cannot produce
(\cref{sec:results_unmodeled}). The second variable thus resolves the
task/observation tension instead of trading it off: it is the structured
version of simply injecting noise.

\paragraph{Mask or mixed selectivity --- a testable distinction.}
The orthogonality that makes both variables recoverable lives in the readout
\emph{weights}, not necessarily in a partition of the cells, and it can be
realised two ways. In the \emph{labelled-line} case the variables occupy
disjoint cell sets --- one subpopulation codes heading, the complementary one
codes $d$ --- and orthogonality is automatic because the readouts share no
cells. In the \emph{mixed-selectivity} case the \emph{same} cells carry both
and the two readouts are orthogonal as weight \emph{vectors} while overlapping
in support: for two neurons, $w_\theta=(1,1)$ and $w_d=(1,-1)$ give
$w_\theta\!\cdot\!w_d=0$ yet every cell contributes to both. The disjoint
mask is the special, sparse case; mixed selectivity --- the general one --- is
what most heading and cortical populations are observed to do. Which holds here
is an empirical question the recording answers directly, with no model: score
each recurrent cell's tuning to heading (its per-neuron information,
\cref{eq:zhd_mi_plugin}) against its forward-swim weight (the regression of
\cref{fig:zhd_blockenc}). If the heading-tuned and forward-tuned cells are
largely \emph{disjoint}, the second variable is a separate sub-population (a
mask); if many single cells score on \emph{both}, it is genuinely multiplexed
onto the heading cells --- the stronger claim, and the one that would make the
dIPN a mixed heading/self-motion code rather than two interleaved
labelled lines. This single per-cell tuning scatter is the cleanest near-term
test of the two-variable hypothesis.

\paragraph{A multi-task objective.}
This is a small generalisation of the model we already have. The single
heading readout (\cref{eq:zhd_readout}) becomes a set of per-variable heads
sharing the same recurrent state,
\begin{equation}
\hat y^{(k)}(t) = \hat W^{(k),\mathrm{out}}\,\sigma\!\big(\hat h(t)\big)
  + \hat b^{(k),\mathrm{out}},
\qquad k \in \{\theta,\ d,\ \dots\},
\label{eq:zhd_multihead}
\end{equation}
where $k=\theta$ is the heading head of \cref{eq:zhd_readout} and $k=d$ is a
\emph{self-motion} head supervised by a leaky accumulator of the measured
forward-swim drive $u^{\mathrm{fwd}}$ (the slow, $\sim$stimulus-independent
behaviour of \cref{sec:results_unmodeled}),
\begin{equation}
d(t) = \int_0^{t} e^{-(t-s)/\tau_d}\, u^{\mathrm{fwd}}(s)\,ds
\qquad (\text{``how far the fish has swum recently''}),
\label{eq:zhd_selfmotion}
\end{equation}
an aperiodic quantity the heading drive does not carry. Training then runs
over all blocks at once, with a mask $m^{(k)}_b\in\{0,1\}$ that turns on each
task only where its variable is actually driven, while the calcium-observation
loss (\cref{eq:zhd_obs}) runs on \emph{every} block,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}
  \Big[\ \sum_k m^{(k)}_b\,\lambda_k\,
        \mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{task},b}
      \ +\ \lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs},b}\ \Big].
\label{eq:zhd_multitask}
\end{equation}
Each block supplies supervision for whatever it excites; the shared,
connectome-locked circuit must represent all of it at once. Setting
$\mathcal{B}=\{\text{Rotations}\}$ and $k=\{\theta\}$ recovers the present
model exactly.

\paragraph{Why this is a measurement, not a free latent.}
The second target $d$ is computed from an \emph{independent} signal (the tail
EMG), not inferred from the calcium it must explain --- so it cannot absorb
arbitrary structure the way an unconstrained latent input would. Two
conditions keep it honest, and both are properties of the \emph{data}, not the
optimiser: the second head must (i) generalise to a block it was not fit on,
and (ii) beat a phase-scrambled surrogate of $d$. There is also a subtler
requirement --- $\theta$ and $d$ must be \emph{decorrelated} in training, or
the two heads collapse onto a single entangled axis instead of two orthogonal
codes. A single block cannot guarantee this (in Rotations, turning is locked
to the grating); the \emph{union} of blocks does --- Gain~/~OMR drive forward
swimming while heading is not rotating, Rotations drives heading --- so the
conditions together dissociate the variables. This decorrelation question is
ultimately about biology: because the circuit is locked to the real
connectome, whether it \emph{can} hold two orthogonal codes given dissociating
input is a falsifiable test of whether the fish's wiring can multiplex at all.

\paragraph{Which blocks are worth wiring in.}
Before any retraining, the per-block encoding diagnostic
(\cref{fig:zhd_blockenc}; the all-blocks analogue of \cref{fig:zhd_obs_control})
regresses the recorded ring-pool $\Delta F/F$ on the signals available in every
block --- the stimulus code and the forward/turning swim --- to show where the
activity is explainable and where forward-swim carries variance the stimulus
does not. Three facts stand out. (i)~\textbf{OMR~Turning is the keystone}: the
recurrent pool is most explainable there ($R^2 = 0.40$, largely stimulus-driven), it
co-drives heading, turning \emph{and} forward at the highest calcium activity,
and it is the longest block ($1200\,\mathrm{s}$). (ii)~\textbf{Forward-swim adds
significant, surrogate-beating variance} in Rotation ($\Delta R^2 \approx
0.09$), Flash ($0.10$), Taxis ($0.08$) and Turning ($0.05$) --- the
second-variable signal is present across conditions, not an artefact of one
block. (iii)~\textbf{Several blocks are near-unexplainable} from the measured
covariates (Gain, Position, Open-Loop and Darkness sit at $\lesssim 0$ held-out
$R^2$): consistent with a large unmeasured slow-state component, these are the
quiet/latent regime rather than task-driving blocks. The minimal set that
exercises all three axes --- heading, turning, forward --- is therefore
\{Rotation, OMR~Turning, Gain\}, with OMR as the anchor.

\paragraph{Caveats and the engineering lift.}
The main work is heterogeneous stimulus encoding: each block has a different
input (rotation velocity, grating coherence, luminance, L/R light, closed-loop
gain), so a unified model needs a common input vector spanning all of them, not
just the heading drive $s(t)$ --- and the continuous drive is currently
reconstructed only for Rotation (and, coarsely, Turning); the other blocks
would have to be lifted from the stimulus log into proper model inputs. Two
blocks are closed-loop (Gain, Position), where the visual input is not cleanly
exogenous because it depends on the fish's own swimming; Random-Dots,
Position, Open-Loop and Darkness carry little ring-pool signal and serve mainly
as the quiet/baseline regime (and, for Darkness, as the cleanest test of
whether the circuit drifts on its own --- the unstructured, route-1 limit of
the same idea). We leave the unified multi-task model to future work; the
present section establishes that the data to constrain it --- nine conditions,
the same neurons throughout, an independent behavioural covariate --- are
already in hand.
