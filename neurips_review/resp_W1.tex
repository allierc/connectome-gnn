\paragraph{W1.}
We agree: the three idealizations are coupled, and the paper never separates them. The question we take from it: how much of the
recovery comes from solving the inverse problem, and how much from handing the
model the generator's own class and discrete map. We ran two new families of
experiments. All runs are single-seed at $\sigma=0.05$.

\paragraph{1. Model-class match.}
We added a slow adaptation current that leaves the hypothesis class in two ways at
once: it is not first order in the observables, and does not decompose into
pairwise messages over the graph:
\[
  \tau_i \dot v_i = -v_i + V^{\mathrm{rest}}_i
  + \sum_j W_{ij}\,\mathrm{ReLU}(v_j) - g_a c_i + I_i ,
  \qquad
  \dot c_i = (v_i - c_i)/\tau_a ,
\]
with $\tau_a = 200$~ms $\gg \tau$, and $c_i$ never observed and never supplied to
either model. Neither model is changed: Eq.~(2) is untouched, $c_i$ is never a
state, input, or learned quantity, and the target is the observed $\dot v_i$
including $-g_a c_i/\tau_i$.

Degradation is graceful and ordered: $V^{\mathrm{rest}}$ first, $W$ next, $\tau$
last, and the oracle matches. A slow per-neuron offset is what $\widehat
V^{\mathrm{rest}}$ absorbs; its residual partly aliases into the recurrent drive
and biases $\widehat W$; $\tau$, read off the local slope of $f_\theta$, is almost
untouched. Relatedly, the priors listed as class-narrowing are not
load-bearing: $\mu_0=0$ in every reported run, and $\mu_1=0$ leaves $\Rw$ at
$0.988$ against $0.987$ for the control.

$20/20$~ms is the matched case: the generator \emph{is} the model's Euler map,
and any finer step makes the observed difference $\dot v + (\Delta t/2)\,\ddot v$,
a neuron-specific bias that tracks the recurrent drive rather than a global
rescaling. Coarse observation is the real limitation, and it reproduces the
published $1/5$-frames row ($\Rw = 0.30$ at $100$~ms). Rollout $r$ stays above
$0.97$ across every cadence while $\Rw$ falls to $0.287$, so held-out prediction
cannot detect the violation. Limitation~2 already flags the shared $\Delta t$;
the sweep shows the binding constraint is
$\Delta t_{\mathrm{obs}} \lesssim \tau$, which we will state there quantitatively.

Known-ODE degrades in step with the GNN, so the cost lies in the
inverse problem, not the hypothesis class: across the conditions tested here the
GNN stays within $0.023$ $\Rw$ of it (the noise-free gap of Tab.~1 is addressed
under Q3). We therefore retain ``on par with an oracle'' as a claim about the
estimator. We also drop ``without assuming the form of the dynamical equation'':
the scalar nonlinearities are learned, but first-order dynamics and additive
pairwise aggregation over the supplied graph are assumed. We remove the clause
rather than reword it.
