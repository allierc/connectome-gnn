Heading-direction circuits Identifiable computation, non-identifiable implementation — an interactive companion to the central-complex & zebrafish manuscripts

As an animal turns, a single bump of activity travels around a recurrently connected ring of neurons and tracks where it is facing — an internal compass built by path integration of angular velocity. Below, the bump is not drawn — it emerges from the recurrent dynamics, live in your browser. Drag the angular velocity and watch heading integrate; then scramble the synaptic weights and watch the computation survive.

Drag angular velocity — the bump rotates and heading accumulates. Set velocity to zero: the bump holds its position — that persistent memory is the attractor.

This is the Known-ODE recurrent model from the manuscript (τ = 0.1 s), reduced to the EPG ring so it runs live. The symmetric cosine term holds a stationary bump; the velocity-gated antisymmetric sin term — the role played anatomically by the left/right PEN populations — shifts it. Heading is read out by population vector, exactly the cos/sin readout the full model is trained on.

Interactive 2 · the degeneracy

Many circuits, one computation

Two circuits run side by side on the same velocity command. Circuit A is the clean ring. Circuit B has its recurrent weights randomly rescaled, edge by edge — a different implementation consistent with the same ring topology. Turn up implementation diversity: the weight matrix visibly scrambles, yet both bumps track the same heading. The task identifies the computation, not a unique circuit.

Verified: the heading readout survives per-edge weight randomness up to ±140% (the bump persists; integration gain stays within a factor of ~1.4). This mirrors the manuscript result that across cross-validation folds many connectome-consistent weight matrices — and even the message-passing GNN with its different mechanism — integrate heading equally well. Breaking the degeneracy requires going beyond the task: confronting the model with measured neural activity (the zebrafish calcium-observation loss).

the science

Two brains, one ring attractor

Fly — central complex. 156 neurons across seven classes (EPG, EPGt, PENa, PENb, Δ7, PEG, ER6). The heading bump lives in the ellipsoid body; PEN neurons inject angular velocity to rotate it. Connectome from Hulse et al. 2021; RNN model from Vafidis et al. 2022.

Fish — anterior hindbrain / dIPN. A GABAergic ring attractor at single-neuron resolution, all-inhibitory under Dale's law, integrating swim-triggered angular impulses (≈ π/4 rad each). Built on Petrucco et al. 2023 and the ZAPBench whole-brain calcium recording.

We lock each synapse to its anatomical sign (Dale's law) and topology, then train constrained RNN and message-passing GNN models on path-/swim-integration under cross-validation. The result is the same in fly and fish: the computation is identifiable, the implementation is not. Optimising the input–output map reliably recovers the ring attractor, but does not pin down a unique circuit.
